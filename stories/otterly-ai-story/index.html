Bootstrapped Into the AI Search Era — and First to Win the Industry’s New Award

Thomas Peham, Klaus-M. Schremser and Josef Trauner built Otterly.AI without venture capital funding, grew it past 30,000 users, and in May 2026 took home Best AI Search Software Solution at the European Search Awards — while being named a Gartner Cool Vendor in 2025.

Three founders, zero venture capital

Otterly.AI was founded by Josef Trauner, Klaus-M. Schremser and Thomas Peham — and, according to the GlobeNewswire announcement of its European Search Awards win, it was “built without venture capital funding.” In a category — AI search visibility — where venture money has poured in on the assumption that the generative era needs a land grab, the bootstrapped origin is a deliberate part of the story.

The trade is the classic one: no outside capital means no outside clock. The team entered the AI-search monitoring space early, built the product on its own economics, and by the time the industry got around to creating awards for the category, Otterly.AI was already positioned as the company to beat. Bootstrapping did not slow the category entry; it funded it.

The bet: AI answers are the new search results

The product thesis is straightforward: as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews become where answers happen, brands need to monitor and optimize their visibility inside AI-generated answers — not just inside classic search results. Otterly.AI is an AI search monitoring platform covering those surfaces, with plans starting at $29 per month and a 7-day free trial.

The market has started to ratify the bet. Otterly.AI was named a Gartner Cool Vendor in 2025, a designation Gartner reserves for emerging companies with genuinely interesting technology. For a bootstrapped team, that kind of third-party validation functions as the credibility that venture backing usually buys — earned, not funded.

Mentions over links: Peham’s reframing

CEO and co-founder Thomas Peham has also done the conceptual work of defining what winning looks like in the new medium. Speaking on the Voices of Search podcast in February 2026, in an episode titled “Lessons from AI startup mistakes,” he argued that brand mentions in AI answers are more commercially valuable than traditional link citations — and that mention-centric optimization is replacing link-centric optimization as the discipline that matters.

It is a direct challenge to two decades of SEO orthodoxy, and it is also product strategy disguised as thought leadership. If the unit of value in AI search is the mention rather than the link, then the entire measurement stack has to be rebuilt around mentions — which is exactly what an AI search monitoring platform sells. Peham is not just describing the category; he is defining its scoreboard.

The industry’s first dedicated award

In May 2026, the category got its institutional moment: Otterly.AI won Best AI Search Software Solution at the European Search Awards 2026. The GlobeNewswire announcement framed it as cementing the company’s status as the award-winning AI search analytics tool for the generative era — the industry’s first dedicated prize for the space, going to a bootstrapped team.

The announcement also put a scale number on the story: more than 30,000 users worldwide. For a company built without venture capital, that user count is the other half of the award narrative — not just best product in a new category, but best product built on its own economics. The two facts together are the story: early entry, real traction, no outside money.

Six platforms, one thesis

The product covers the full spread of the new search surface: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot. For each, it shows brand mentions, citations, and share of voice against competitors, with recommendations for getting cited more — the operational layer under Peham's mention-centric thesis. The company reports more than 30,000 users worldwide, and Gartner named it a Cool Vendor in 2025.

The go-to-market thinking is as deliberate as the product. On the Voices of Search podcast, Peham laid out a founder-led sales methodology for AI startups: validate product-market fit through direct founder contact before hiring a sales leader, and build scalable enterprise deal frameworks only after the founder has closed the first ones personally. It is the same practitioner-first instinct that runs through the whole story — the founders learned AI search visibility by doing it, then productized what they learned.

Why bootstrapped matters here

There is a reason the bootstrapping detail keeps surfacing. The AI search visibility category has attracted heavy venture funding on the thesis that the generative era requires a land grab — whoever maps AI answers first wins. Otterly.AI's counter-thesis is that the category is won by product depth, not capital depth: a team that built the monitoring because they needed it, validated it founder-to-customer, and let partnerships carry it into the enterprise.

Whether that thesis holds for the whole category is an open question. But the European Search Awards jury — in a category created specifically for this space — picked the bootstrapped team over the funded ones. In a market that loves a land grab, that is a result worth noticing.

Product-led, founder-sold

The go-to-market matches the origin. On the February 2026 Voices of Search episode “The biggest myth about AI search visibility,” Peham described Otterly.AI as a product-led AI search optimization platform serving enterprise clients through strategic partnerships — a hybrid motion: the product sells itself to the long tail while partnerships open enterprise doors.

And the enterprise motion itself is founder-driven. Peham described a founder-led sales methodology with scalable enterprise deal frameworks, and was explicit about validating product-market fit through direct founder contact — the founders themselves in the sales conversations, learning what the market will pay for. It is the bootstrapped playbook applied to enterprise: no SDR army, just founders, frameworks, and a product that had already earned its users.

The playbook

  1. Bootstrap the category entry — Entered AI search visibility early and built it “without venture capital funding” — no outside clock, economics that compound.
  2. Sell product-led, partner into enterprise — A product-led platform serving enterprise clients through strategic partnerships — self-serve growth below, partnerships above.
  3. Founders validate PMF in person — Founder-led sales methodology with scalable enterprise deal frameworks; product-market fit validated through direct founder contact.
  4. Reframe the metric the category optimizes — Pehm’s thesis: brand mentions in AI answers beat traditional link citations — mention-centric optimization replaces link-centric, and the product measures the new unit.
  5. Collect category-defining proof — European Search Awards 2026 Best AI Search Software Solution plus Gartner Cool Vendor 2025 — third-party validation that venture backing usually buys, earned instead.
