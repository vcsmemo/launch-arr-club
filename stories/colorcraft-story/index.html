How ColorCraft turned one-line book ideas into $2K a month — built at night, grown by search

John Kirby leads large software programs by day and builds products by night. His AI coloring-book maker took 18 months to reach $2,000 a month — then grew 675% in a single quarter.

Built at night

John Kirby has a full-time day job leading large software programs. By night he builds products — and he also renovates and runs short-term rentals in North Carolina. ColorCraft is one of several side projects, and the one that stuck.

The product is simple to describe: you type a one-line book idea, and it draws the pages. It started with coloring books for people who self-publish on Amazon KDP, and now also makes storybooks, comics and puzzle books, exporting a print-ready PDF with a cover wrap sized to the page count, so the files pass KDP's checks. Plans run from $9 to $119 a month after a 7-day trial.

Kirby says he barely wrote any code for it. The whole app was vibe-coded with AI, using Cursor, and built on Supabase, Vercel and Stripe, with OpenAI's image API drawing the pages at about half a cent per image. He launched in May 2025. About 18 months later it was earning around $2,000 a month — slow at first, then fast.

The free tier was the problem

The turning point was not a feature. It was the free tier. Kirby kept shrinking free credits because he feared abuse and cost — then he checked his numbers. People who tried once or twice almost never paid. People who made 10 to 30 attempts paid at 14.5%. There were no casual paying customers: either users got deeply hooked or they walked.

In May 2026 he dropped the free tier for a card-gated 7-day trial with 24 free pages — enough to complete a whole book. He says the trial costs him about $0.12 in image generation against a $14-a-month payback. The funnel he posted that month told the story: 2,450 signups, 1,373 people who generated something, 80 who paid.

He also stopped selling AI images and started selling finished books. Self-publishers, he says, stitch together four to seven tools to ship one book. ColorCraft puts them in one place — and the trial is designed so a buyer finishes one real result before the card is charged.

Search did the selling

ColorCraft's marketing budget is close to zero. Instead of ads, Kirby gives away small free tools that search engines find: KDP spine and royalty calculators, and hundreds of free printable pages. In September 2026 he posted on X that he had not marketed the product in months — and MRR kept going up.

The growth came in a burst at the end. In November he added printed copies sent to your door and a bookstore where authors sell printed books. On 8 November he posted that monthly revenue had passed $2,006, a 675% increase in just three months. Stripe data checked two days later showed $1,972 in monthly recurring revenue from 107 subscriptions, $2,181 in the last 30 days and $7,159 earned all-time.

It is worth saying plainly what the numbers are: revenue, not profit. AI images, hosting and payment fees come out first, and the product took about 18 months to reach roughly $2,000 a month. Kirby works in software, which helps when reviewing code an AI writes — and most small apps make little or nothing. The realistic first goal, as he puts it, is ten people who finish one result, then five paying users.

The climb is visible in the snapshots. An indie newsletter recorded $1,632 in MRR on 2 November; six days later Kirby posted that revenue had passed $2,006. The 675% figure he quoted covers the three months to November — from a base so small that a few dozen new subscribers moved the line sharply. It is the shape of a product finding its buyers late, not a launch spike.

November also brought the first moves beyond the PDF. Kirby added printed copies sent to your door and a bookstore where authors sell printed books — the same finished-book logic, extended from a file to a physical object. The free calculators and printable pages stay at the top of the funnel, doing the job ads would do, while the trial does the selling.

The numbers

ColorCraft's revenue is verified by payment data, not by a reporter: its Stripe account is read directly, showing $1,972 in MRR and $2,181 in the last 30 days as of 10 November 2026. The $2,006 figure and the 675% growth claim are Kirby's own posts on X from 8 November. His signup and conversion numbers come from the same posts.

What the numbers do not show is the cost side. Each AI image costs about half a cent, so a 24-page trial costs about 12 cents — cheap, but not free, and the bill scales with every page a user generates. KDP has its own rules too: Amazon asks publishers to disclose AI-generated content and can remove books that break its content guidelines.

The business is small, slow-built and still growing without ads — a one-person product where the distribution is a handful of free calculators and the conversion is a trial long enough to finish a book. That is the whole playbook, and it is the reason the MRR line kept climbing while Kirby was not marketing at all.

The playbook

  1. Pick buyers who already pay — KDP self-publishers already spend money to put books out. Find one group like that, and list the tools they mention for one task.
  2. Let the trial finish one result — A trial too small to finish anything converts nobody. Give enough room to complete a whole book — then charge.
  3. Give away tools search finds — Free calculators and printable pages bring buyers in for free. Build one or two that your buyers already search for.
  4. Measure what one use really costs — Check the AI bill before setting prices: at half a cent an image, a 24-page trial costs 12 cents. Price from the real number.
