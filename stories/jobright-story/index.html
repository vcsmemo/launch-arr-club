How Jobright hit $8M ARR: the AI headhunter for the overlooked

Eric Cheng sold his last SaaS company, spent the pandemic helping strangers find jobs across China, and realized AI could scale what he did by hand. Jobright is now at $8M ARR — growing 30x year over year.

By Eric Cheng, Ethan Zheng · 2026-10-14 · View on MilestoneWins →

Ten strangers, one road trip, one idea

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Eric Cheng did something unusual for a successful founder. After selling his content collaboration SaaS company, he traveled across China — not to the tech hubs, but to underdeveloped cities, where he met dozens of young people struggling to land jobs in a global slowdown.

Cheng started doing what came naturally: asking about their skillsets, suggesting career options, making introductions. He ended up helping ten of them secure jobs — by hand, one conversation at a time. What struck him wasn't the gratitude; it was the gap. As he later told TechCrunch: they were talented, but their understanding of the job market was limited. They didn't know what jobs existed or what they could possibly do.

The thought experiment that became Jobright: what if everyone had a seasoned headhunter who knew them well and could recommend opportunities beyond their knowledge? For years that was impossible to scale. Then ChatGPT arrived, and Cheng saw the missing piece. Large language models could do what he had done on that road trip — understand a person, understand the market, and match the two — for millions of people at once.

It's a founding story with a rare quality: the insight came from service, not strategy. Cheng wasn't analyzing TAM sheets; he was helping strangers. The business emerged from noticing that the help scaled poorly and that AI had just fixed the scaling problem.

The immigrant insight nobody else saw

Cheng, himself an immigrant and a former early engineer at Box, understood a specific pain that most job platforms ignore. Job searching is daunting for anyone — but for non-immigrants in the US, it's a maze with a timer. You compete on resume merits while also hunting the subset of postings that sponsor work visas. Lose your job and you have roughly 60 days to land another before you must leave the country.

Jobright's H-1B filter — built on USCIS data showing which companies historically sponsor visas — became an instant hit among job seekers from India and China. Around 30% of Jobright's users are foreign workers, a group Cheng says has long been overlooked by mainstream platforms.

This is classic wedge strategy: find the users whose pain is most acute and serve them disproportionately well. The H-1B filter wasn't the whole product, but it was the reason thousands of people tried Jobright first — and telling a desperate audience you built something specifically for them is the cheapest marketing that exists.

The wedge worked. Jobright amassed 50,000 registered users from its April launch without spending on marketing, and the company has since grown to over 520,000 professionals — expanding 30x year over year. Overlooked audiences, it turns out, are often just underserved markets waiting for someone to notice.

AI-native, not AI-wrapped

Jobright competes in one of the most crowded categories in AI — job search assistants — against players like YC-backed Simplify and the looming shadow of LinkedIn itself. Cheng's differentiation argument is architectural: Jobright is AI-native, not an AI wrapper.

The distinction matters. A wrapper bolts a chatbot onto a traditional job board. Jobright's AI agent acts as a headhunter: it interprets a candidate's experience, scores positions by compatibility — a feature used by 60% of users daily — recommends jobs candidates wouldn't have considered, and even identifies LinkedIn contacts likely to offer referrals through alumni and former-coworker matching.

When asked whether LinkedIn could simply copy the features, Cheng's answer was structural: LinkedIn is fundamentally a social network with an ad-based business model driven by clicks rather than accuracy. Fully embracing AI would require toppling that model. Jobright has no such conflict — AI defines the data, the recommendations, and the experience, because there's nothing else to protect.

The positioning against Simplify is equally deliberate. While Simplify serves internship-seeking students and fresh graduates, Jobright targets mid- to senior-level professionals whose problem isn't applying to more jobs but finding the right strategy. Same category, different customer, different product — the kind of segmentation that lets two companies win simultaneously.

From $4.5M to $8M ARR: the funding arc

Jobright's capital path reflects its momentum. An early $4.5 million round led by Lanchi Ventures, with UpHonest Ventures and Source Code Capital participating, funded the initial build. A later $3.2 million financing led by Translink Capital — with participation from HR Tech Investments, the venture arm affiliated with Indeed — coincided with the launch of Jobright Agent, the company's flagship AI career agent.

Jobright Agent marked the product's evolution from copilot to autopilot: an agent that finds, customizes, and submits applications on behalf of job seekers. The company reports it delivers twice the interviews while cutting job-search time by 80% compared to traditional boards. The framing — for decades, job boards made you work; now our agent works for you — captures the searchless future Cheng is betting on.

The team itself is part of the story: serial entrepreneurs and engineers from Box, Twitter, Google, Amazon, and TikTok. Cheng's own path — Box early engineer, SaaS founder, exit, then the pandemic road trip — gave him both the technical credibility and the lived experience to lead it.

At $8M ARR and 30x year-over-year growth, Jobright has outgrown the scrappy-immigrant-tool narrative without abandoning it. The H-1B filter that won the first 50,000 users still embodies the company's thesis: the best AI products don't serve everyone equally — they serve someone specifically, deeply, and first. Scale comes after.

The playbook

  1. Serve the most desperate users first — The H-1B filter targeted foreign workers on a 60-day clock — acute pain converts without marketing spend.
  2. Turn lived experience into product insight — Cheng's road trip helping strangers find jobs became the product spec; the AI just scaled what he did by hand.
  3. Be AI-native, not AI-wrapped — When the tech defines data, recommendations, and UX — not just a chatbot layer — incumbents can't copy you without breaking their business model.
  4. Segment the crowded category — While Simplify chases students, Jobright owns mid-to-senior professionals. Same market, different customer, room for both.
  5. Evolve copilot into autopilot — Jobright Agent submits applications for you — moving from assistance to agency doubled interviews and cut search time 80%.
