The 'Volkswagen of SEO': how gamification took Morningscore to $720K ARR

Karsten Madsen learned SEO running his own agency, then turned it into a gamified tool where customers complete 'missions' to raise a 0–100 SEO Health score — and grew from $420K to $720K ARR in three years.

A love story with SEO

Karsten Madsen is honest about where he started: he was, in his own words, really bad at school and figured he would just do business to get by. It wasn't ambition that pulled him into SEO — it was necessity. He learned it running his own agency, grinding through the craft the way most practitioners do, and he turned out to be good at it. He ended up ranking big clients high in Google, and the discipline that had beaten him in school turned out to be something he genuinely loved.

For him, as he later said on the Reditus podcast, SEO was a love story. That framing matters, because it explains the emotional engine behind Morningscore. This wasn't a founder who picked a market after a spreadsheet exercise. He fell for the discipline itself — the feedback loop of doing the work and watching rankings climb. The love story phase of his career was spent doing SEO for other people, and it was inside that work that the product idea quietly formed.

The insight came from watching his own clients. They were confused — not stupid, but lost inside the machinery of SEO. The existing tools were built for experts, full of jargon and dashboards that assumed you already knew what you were doing. Madsen watched beginners drown in data and decided the industry needed something radically simpler: an SEO tool anyone could pick up and use.

Gamifying SEO with missions

Morningscore's answer was gamification. Instead of a wall of metrics, users get an SEO Health score from 0 to 100 and a series of 'missions' — concrete tasks that nudge the score upward. Fix a broken link, improve a title tag, publish a page: each completed mission moves the needle, and the needle is visible. SEO stops feeling like homework and starts feeling like progress.

Madsen describes Morningscore as the Volkswagen of SEO — a tool for everyone, not a luxury car for specialists. That's not a throwaway analogy; it's the entire product strategy. Where enterprise SEO tools compete on the depth of their crawl data and the sophistication of their reporting, Morningscore competes on approachability. The pitch is disarmingly simple: teach anyone to get results with SEO in 3 months instead of 3 years.

The company has since expanded into GEO and AI visibility tools for the ChatGPT era, but the core idea hasn't changed: make SEO feel like progress, not homework. And the culture matches the product — the team wears silver space suits at work, as Madsen shared on the Reditus podcast. It's a company that takes playfulness seriously, because playfulness is the product.

$420K to $720K: three years of compounding

In December 2021, Morningscore was doing $420K ARR — a solid business built, in Starter Story's words, on fun game mechanics. The 2021 Starter Story interview laid out the mechanics: the Health score, the missions, the simplification strategy. It was already a real company with real recurring revenue, not a prototype with a pitch deck.

A year later, the 2022 update showed revenue had grown 34% to $575K. Madsen was candid about the number: far from our target, but still solid considering everything we went through. That candor became a signature of the company's public updates — targets missed, growth real, context honest. It's the kind of transparency that reads like confidence.

By 2023, ARR had grown another 25% to reach $720K — 10% off target, but still solid considering the shaky waters, as Madsen put it. Three years, three updates, $420K to $720K: a compounding machine running on a lean operation. Along the way the company won its first major startup award. The stated target at the time was to reach $200K MRR through affiliate partnerships and SEO — a bet that the same channels that grew the tool could scale it further.

Content as the engine

Morningscore practices what it preaches: the company's own growth has leaned heavily on SEO and affiliate marketing, using its own tool to rank for the keywords its customers search for. It's the classic dogfooding loop — every ranking Morningscore wins with its own software is a proof point for the product, and every affiliate partner is a distribution channel that costs nothing upfront.

That organic engine shows in the numbers. Three consecutive years of growth at 25–34% per year took the company from $420K to $720K ARR without the kind of funding round or hype cycle that defines most SaaS stories. No dramatic pivot, no acquisition, no viral moment — just the compounding effect of a tool that ranks because the people building it know how ranking works.

The gamification angle also feeds the marketing. 'We created a $420K/year SEO tool with fun game mechanics' is itself a story that travels — the 2021 Starter Story interview's title was the growth loop in miniature. A distinctive product gets covered; coverage drives trials; trials become the missions-complete testimonials that fuel more coverage.

What comes next

Morningscore started at $69 per month with a 14-day free trial, and the pricing philosophy matches the product: accessible to beginners, honest about what you get, no enterprise pricing page with a 'contact us' button. The Volkswagen comparison extends to the price tag — a car for the people, not a luxury product with a luxury price.

The expansion into AI visibility tools signals where Madsen sees the market going. Search is no longer just Google — it's ChatGPT, Perplexity, and whatever comes next. A gamified 'visibility score' translates naturally to that world; the missions just change from backlinks to the things that make AI engines cite you.

The bet is the same one the company has made since day one: most people don't want an SEO expert's dashboard. They want to know what to do next, do it, and watch a score go up. Three years of growth — and a love story that started in an agency — suggest that bet was right.

The playbook

  1. Gamify the boring part — Morningscore replaced the expert dashboard with a 0–100 SEO Health score and 'missions' — tasks users complete to raise the score. Turned SEO from homework into a game, per the 2021 Starter Story interview.
  2. Position as the 'Volkswagen of SEO' — Madsen's own positioning (2023 Starter Story update): a tool for everyone, not a luxury product for specialists. The everyman positioning carved out space beneath enterprise SEO tools.
  3. Dogfood your own SEO — The company's stated growth target was to reach $200K MRR via affiliate partnerships and SEO — using the same organic channels the product helps customers win.
  4. Sell the shortcut, honestly — The pitch: teach anyone to get SEO results in 3 months instead of 3 years (Reditus podcast). A concrete time-based promise, not vague 'rank higher' claims.
  5. Stay lean through shaky waters — Two consecutive updates (2022, 2023) missed internal targets but grew ARR 34% and 25% anyway — compounding on a profitable base rather than raising to chase faster growth.
