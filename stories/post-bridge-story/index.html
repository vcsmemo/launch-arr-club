How Post Bridge hit $48K MRR posting to 10 platforms at once

Jack Friks built a scheduler to stop re-posting his own videos by hand — then turned down a $1M offer, turned down YC, and let AI agents do the posting.

By Jack Friks ↗ · 2026-11-18 · View on MilestoneWins →
$48.5KMRR, mid-November 2026
1,600Active subscriptions
$582KAll-time revenue
10Platforms from one dashboard

An hour a day, re-posting by hand

Jack Friks was already deep in the indie maker life when the pain became obvious. He was posting short-form video every day to grow Curiosity Quench, his consumer app that later passed 100,000 downloads — and the work took about an hour a day, half of it spent manually copying the same video into Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and every other platform. The schedulers on the market felt expensive and bloated for what he actually needed, which was simple: write once, publish everywhere.

He first mentioned the idea in a September 2024 build log, validated it with mockups before writing much code, then built the first version in roughly a month. Post Bridge launched in October 2024 with a hard paywall and no free tier — a deliberate filter for people who valued the tool enough to pay. It did one job: publish the same content to multiple accounts and platforms from one dashboard. He ran it under Jack Ingram Media Inc. in Ontario, Canada, and answered support himself, as he still does.

The offer he turned down

Early numbers were modest. Friks reported reaching roughly $6,000–$7,000 in monthly recurring revenue within the first several months, with over 500 customers — and then growth plateaued for a stretch. This was not his first attempt at making money online. Before learning to code in earnest in 2023, Friks — then posting under Jack Ingram — had spent years on YouTube channels, print-on-demand, affiliate blogs and cryptocurrency content and projects. He has been open that most of those efforts produced modest or temporary results, and that the crypto period in particular ended with significant losses after taxes and market declines. Curiosity Quench was the first product that gained real traction through short-form video — and Post Bridge was the tool he built so he could keep doing that marketing without the daily friction.

In spring 2025 he publicly declined a $1 million acquisition offer, noting at the time that the business was probably worth less than the offer, and that the attention from saying no appeared to help. An eight-month period of limited growth followed anyway.

He has also described turning down Y Combinator. In late 2025 he posted that Post Bridge was entering its second year with no outside investors beyond family support — fully bootstrapped, still a one-person operation. Mornings start with support, then product work; he continues to ship other apps, including the couples app Lovelee, on the side. The independence was the point: no investors meant no pressure to grow the wrong way, and every decision stayed with the person who used the product daily.

The AI-agent turn

In 2026 the positioning shifted toward AI agents, and that is when the curve bent. Post Bridge shipped a REST API, an npm CLI and an MCP server so tools like Claude and ChatGPT can schedule and publish on a user's behalf. It is listed in the Claude connector directory and as a ChatGPT plugin — the ChatGPT listing appeared in early September 2026, and Friks documented a spike immediately after, with four consecutive daily signup records well above the prior average of about 50 a day. Guides for posting from ChatGPT, Claude and other models followed.

The product itself kept expanding beyond the original one-dashboard job: per-platform caption overrides, a calendar, bulk upload, analytics, workspaces and team features on higher plans. Pricing stayed simple and stayed tied to the number of connected accounts rather than per channel, with unlimited posts on paid plans — Marketer starts at $39 a month for 15 accounts. The agent interfaces extended the same core job instead of turning the tool into an enterprise suite, and the customers who arrived through ChatGPT and Claude were exactly the people who wanted posting to happen without them touching it.

The numbers that compounded

By mid-2026 the picture had changed. Friks reported monthly recurring revenue roughly doubling to around $35,000 — in one May 2026 update he listed Post Bridge at about $36,000 for the month as part of a broader personal revenue total across products. He has shared detailed cost breakdowns on X: infrastructure including Supabase, Vercel, Trigger.dev, Unkey and the X API totals under $2,000 a month against $35,000-plus in MRR, implying gross margins above 90% and net margins in the high 80s after payment fees.

By mid-November 2026 the business stood at approximately $48,500 in monthly recurring revenue, with roughly 1,600 active subscriptions and about $582,000 in all-time revenue — past $500,000 in annualised revenue. Support stays with the founder: the about page notes that emails go to Jack and that most replies land within a few hours, and there is no separate support team. Growth has come primarily from building in public on X, the short-form video formats he already used for his own apps, and word of mouth among creators and founders, with Product Hunt and Indie Hackers contributing early visibility. He has been explicit that he does not treat paid acquisition as the core engine. The through-line is the one he wrote himself: scratch a real itch you have yourself, charge early, keep the surface area manageable — and keep shipping.

The playbook

  1. Scratch your own itch, daily — He built it because he was losing an hour a day re-posting his own videos. The product existed to serve its founder first.
  2. Charge from day one — Hard paywall and no free tier at launch. The paywall filtered for people who valued the tool enough to pay.
  3. Say no to the wrong money — He declined a $1M acquisition offer and Y Combinator. Staying independent kept the product focused on its users.
  4. Let the platforms come to you — A REST API, CLI and MCP server put Post Bridge inside Claude and ChatGPT — and that is where the signups now come from.
  5. Price by accounts, not by posts — Unlimited posts on every plan; pricing scales with connected accounts, the thing customers actually understand.
