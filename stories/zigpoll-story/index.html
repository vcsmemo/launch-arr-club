How Zigpoll hit $142K MRR as a one-person SaaS

Jason Zigelbaum runs Zigpoll — $142K MRR of Shopify survey software — entirely solo, with AI coding agents as his team and the Shopify App Store as his sales force.

The Shopify App Store flywheel

Zigpoll is a contextual micro-survey tool for eCommerce: post-purchase attribution surveys, NPS, and on-site polls that trigger on purchase events, with responses tied to real customer data — order value, segments, lifetime value — instead of anonymous visitors. It is the kind of unglamorous infrastructure software that rarely makes headlines and quietly prints money.

The distribution is the strategy. Roughly 90% of the business flows through the Shopify App Store, where reviews are the algorithm: merchants comparison-shop on ratings, and a well-reviewed app compounds installs without ad spend. Zigelbaum's stated growth levers, in an October 2026 Niche Pursuits interview, are reviews, churn reduction, onboarding, and customer feedback. No flashy campaigns — just the unsexy work, repeated.

The product design fits the channel. Post-purchase surveys answer the question every Shopify merchant asks — where did my customers actually come from? — at the exact moment the data is freshest. Contextual triggering plus real order data makes the answers actionable, which makes merchants stay, which makes reviews accumulate. The flywheel turns itself.

Doubling every year since 2022

The numbers tell the compounding story: roughly $10–15K MRR in 2022, past $1M ARR earlier in 2026, and about $142,000 in MRR — roughly $1.7 million annualized — by October 2026. That is approximately 100% year-over-year growth sustained across four years, with a stated goal of $2M ARR by year end.

There is no hockey stick here, and that is the point. Zigelbaum attributes the growth to consistent, practical improvements — product tweaks, better onboarding, pricing experiments, support quality — compounding over years. It is the least exciting growth story imaginable, which is why almost nobody copies it and why it works.

The next chapter is deliberate: from $2M toward $4M in annual revenue, via annual plans and moving upmarket. The same playbook — better product, less churn, higher-value customers — applied to a larger prize.

AI as the entire team

Zigpoll is a one-person company, and Zigelbaum is explicit about how: AI coding agents across product development, design, marketing, and data analysis. The founder's job description has collapsed to judgment and taste — the agents do the implementation across every function that used to require hires.

The one thing he hasn't delegated is the thing agents can't do: he still personally answers customer support email. Support is his research lab — every ticket is a feature request, a churn signal, or a testimonial in disguise. It's the same instinct that made reviews a growth channel: stay close to the customer, and the product markets itself.

This is the new indie-hacker stack made concrete. Not AI will replace teams as a slogan, but a $1.7M ARR business where one human plus agents out-executes companies with headcount. The constraint isn't labor anymore; it's the founder's ability to direct it.

The playbook

  1. Own one distribution channel completely — Roughly 90% of revenue comes through the Shopify App Store. Reviews compound into installs — no ad spend required.
  2. Treat churn reduction as a growth channel — Onboarding, pricing, and feedback loops drive more growth than any campaign. Zigelbaum's words: consistent, practical improvements.
  3. Use AI agents as staff, stay human where it counts — AI agents handle development, design, marketing, and data analysis. The founder personally answers support — the one job agents can't do.
  4. Move upmarket on purpose — Annual plans and higher tiers are the deliberate path from $2M toward $4M ARR.
  5. Compound boring improvements — $10K to $142K MRR with no hockey stick — just iteration, repeated for four years.
