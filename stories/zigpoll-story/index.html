How Zigpoll hit $142K MRR as a one-person SaaS

Jason Zigelbaum runs Zigpoll — $142K MRR of Shopify survey software — entirely solo, with AI coding agents as his team and the Shopify App Store as his sales force.

The survey that arrives at the right moment

Zigpoll is a contextual micro-survey tool for eCommerce: post-purchase attribution surveys, NPS, and on-site polls that trigger on purchase events, with responses tied to real customer data — order value, segments, lifetime value — instead of anonymous visitors. It is the kind of unglamorous infrastructure software that rarely makes headlines and quietly prints money.

The product sits on top of a genuine merchant pain. Since Apple's iOS 14.5 kneecapped third-party tracking, Shopify merchants have been flying increasingly blind on attribution — ad platforms claim credit for the same sale, and nobody knows which half of the budget works. A one-question survey at the moment of purchase (how did you hear about us?) answered by a verified buyer and attached to a real order is the cheapest reliable attribution signal left. That is zero-party data, collected at the exact instant of maximum intent.

Contextual triggering is the difference between signal and noise. An email survey three days later gets ignored; a micro-survey embedded in the purchase flow, pre-filled with order context, gets answered. Zigpoll's bet is that surveys don't fail because merchants dislike data — they fail because they're deployed at the wrong moment, to the wrong person, with no connection to revenue. Fix the context and the response quality follows.

The Shopify App Store flywheel

The distribution is the strategy. Roughly 90% of Zigpoll's business flows through the Shopify App Store, where reviews function as the algorithm: merchants comparison-shop on ratings, and a well-reviewed app compounds installs without ad spend. Zigelbaum's stated growth levers, in an October 2026 Niche Pursuits interview, are reviews, churn reduction, onboarding, and customer feedback. No flashy campaigns — just the unsexy work, repeated until it compounds.

The App Store dynamic rewards exactly Zigpoll's shape. A focused tool with a clear job-to-be-done earns specific, keyword-rich reviews (attribution survey, post-purchase poll), which rank for the exact searches high-intent merchants type. Each new review slightly improves conversion on the listing, which drives more installs, which drives more reviews. Competing against that flywheel with paid acquisition is brutally expensive; riding it is nearly free.

This is why the product stays narrow. Every feature Zigelbaum ships — better attribution questions, tighter Shopify data integration, cleaner onboarding — feeds the same loop: happier merchants, better reviews, more installs. Breadth would dilute the review corpus and the positioning. Depth compounds it.

Doubling every year since 2022

The numbers tell the compounding story: roughly $10–15K MRR in 2022, past $1M ARR earlier in 2026, and about $142,000 in MRR — roughly $1.7 million annualized — by October 2026. That is approximately 100% year-over-year growth sustained across four years, with a stated goal of $2M ARR by year end. From a ~$12K midpoint in 2022 to $142K is roughly an 11x in four years — doubling, every year, on schedule.

There is no hockey stick here, and that is the point. Zigelbaum attributes the growth to consistent, practical improvements — product tweaks, better onboarding, pricing experiments, support quality — compounding over years. It is the least exciting growth story imaginable, which is why almost nobody copies it and why it works. Most founders quit boring channels in month three; Zigelbaum is still iterating in year four.

The next chapter is deliberate rather than explosive: from $2M toward $4M in annual revenue, via annual plans and moving upmarket to larger merchants. The same playbook — better product, less churn, higher-value customers — applied to a larger prize. Annual plans do double duty: cash up front and a full year to prove value before the renewal decision.

AI as the entire team

Zigpoll is a one-person company, and Zigelbaum is explicit about how: AI coding agents across product development, design, marketing, and data analysis. The founder's job description has collapsed to judgment and taste — the agents do the implementation across every function that used to require hires. A solo founder in 2026 can ship like a small team in 2022, and Zigpoll is the receipts.

The one thing he hasn't delegated is the thing agents can't do: he still personally answers customer support email. Support is his research lab — every ticket is a feature request, a churn signal, or a testimonial in disguise. It's the same instinct that made reviews a growth channel: stay close to the customer, and the product markets itself. Founders who outsource support outsource their best source of truth.

The moat here is structural, not technological. Surveys are not hard to build; what's hard is four years of embedded workflow — merchants whose attribution reporting, NPS tracking, and post-purchase flows run through Zigpoll don't switch over a slightly prettier UI. Add a compounding review corpus and a cost structure with exactly one salary, and you get a business that is profitable at every stage of its growth. This is the new indie-hacker stack made concrete: one human plus agents out-executing companies with headcount, because the constraint isn't labor anymore — it's the founder's ability to direct it.

The playbook

  1. Own one distribution channel completely — Roughly 90% of revenue comes through the Shopify App Store. Reviews compound into installs — no ad spend required.
  2. Treat churn reduction as a growth channel — Onboarding, pricing, and feedback loops drive more growth than any campaign. Zigelbaum's words: consistent, practical improvements.
  3. Use AI agents as staff, stay human where it counts — AI agents handle development, design, marketing, and data analysis. The founder personally answers support — the one job agents can't do.
  4. Move upmarket on purpose — Annual plans and higher tiers are the deliberate path from $2M toward $4M ARR.
  5. Compound boring improvements — $10K to $142K MRR with no hockey stick — just iteration, repeated for four years.
