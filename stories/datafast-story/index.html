How DataFast hit $31K a month as a one-person analytics business

Marc Lou's revenue-first analytics tool turns marketing channels into money answers — and its subscriptions kept compounding while his one-time bestseller faded.

By Marc Lou ↗ · 2026-11-17 · View on MilestoneWins →

Revenue, not pageviews

Most analytics tools count visitors. DataFast counts money. Install it in under a minute, connect Stripe, LemonSqueezy, Polar or Shopify, and it attributes revenue to the marketing channels that produced it — showing which channels bring paying customers, not just pageviews.

Goals, funnels, visitor journeys and live visitor intelligence sit on top of that one idea: revenue per visitor, not traffic for its own sake. The tool also tracks social media mentions and X link attribution, and ships an API, CLI and MCP access so AI assistants can query the data. The pitch is written for founders who run ads, post content and sponsor newsletters, then wonder which of it actually worked. DataFast is loved by 28,350 users, and plans start at $9 a month — traffic-based pricing for the number of events, with a 14-day free trial and no card required, and two months free on yearly billing.

Built in public, in front of 127,000 people

DataFast was built the way Marc Lou builds everything: in public, in front of 127,000+ followers on X. The homepage says so plainly — he is building DataFast in front of 127,000+ people on Twitter — and that audience is the distribution strategy.

The audience was not bought; it was earned one post at a time. Marc Lou was a software engineer with a regular SaaS job who started building side projects on weekends, and he gained roughly 55,000 followers in two years by posting daily progress, revenue numbers and product launches. Every feature, fix and revenue number becomes a post, and every post reaches people who already trust the builder. By the time a launch happens, the customers are already watching. It is the same playbook that carried his earlier products: post the progress, post the numbers, post the launch — and let the audience do the rest.

That is also why the product's own marketing writes itself. A founder who attributes revenue to channels is exactly the customer for a tool that attributes revenue to channels, and the people watching the build are founders with the same problem.

None of it was quick. The profile of his journey is framed as 36 failed startups before the winners — years of side projects that went nowhere, each one teaching something the next one used. ShipFast was the breakout, a Next.js boilerplate that lets founders launch a paid SaaS in days instead of weeks, and it funded everything after it. DataFast is what the second act looks like: less spectacular than a boilerplate spike, more durable than one.

The recurring engine that outlasted ShipFast

The interesting number is not just the $31,103 DataFast made in October 2026. It is what happened around it. Marc Lou's one-time bestseller, the ShipFast boilerplate, peaked at roughly $141,000 a month and has since faded to about $1,400 a month as its audience finished buying. One-time sales spike and decay; subscriptions compound.

DataFast went the other way. Described at $30,000 MRR in November 2026, it was the second-largest of 16 income streams that added up to $107,142 for October — the month Marc Lou called a personal record, with 90% profit margins and zero employees. The recurring model did what it is supposed to do: while the one-time product faded, the subscription product kept growing a little every month, until it was the reliable line on the card.

The contrast is the lesson of his whole portfolio. The November 2026 snapshot put the portfolio at roughly $70,000–$80,000 a month, led by the newer recurring products while ShipFast faded. Across 28 Stripe-verified indie products studied that quarter, the founders who focused on recurring workflow utilities built assets that kept paying — and commanded a median 3.9x profit multiple on acquisition marketplaces. DataFast is the case study inside that statistic: a boring, recurring, workflow-shaped product that compounds instead of spiking.

One person, 36 startups, no employees

Marc Lou is a French solo founder with no employees and, by his November 2026 newsletter, 36 startups behind him and $3M earned across them — after years of failed attempts that taught him the model. DataFast is the compounding asset inside that portfolio: the product that keeps paying while the experiments around it come and go.

The model only works because the costs stay tiny: one person, no team, no office, distribution that is mostly his own X account, and a newsletter, Just Ship It, that carries the story to the same audience. At roughly 90% margins, a $31,000 month is nearly all profit — and the next month starts from a subscriber base, not from zero. That is the whole bet: build in public, charge monthly, keep the experiments cheap, and let the compounding do the work.

It is also a bet on patience. DataFast did not spike to $30,000 a month; it accumulated there, one retained subscriber at a time, while louder products rose and fell around it. For a solo founder, that is the point: the product that compounds is worth more than the product that trends.

The playbook

  1. Sell the outcome, not the metric — Pageviews don't pay salaries. DataFast answers one question — which channels bring paying customers — and charges for that answer.
  2. Make distribution the product launch — Every build step was posted to 127,000+ followers. The audience arrived before the launch did, so launch day was a formality.
  3. Prefer recurring over one-time — One-time boilerplate sales peaked near $141K a month, then faded to about $1.4K. DataFast's subscriptions compounded to about $30K a month instead.
  4. Keep the portfolio small and cheap — 36 experiments, most of them small. A few compounders — DataFast among them — pay for the rest of the portfolio.
  5. Price for the job — $9 a month to start, priced by events, 14-day trial with no card required. The cheapest way to answer the revenue question wins the signup.
