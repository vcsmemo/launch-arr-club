The Free Plan Was the Whole Marketing Budget: How Marie Martens and Filip Minev Bootstrapped Tally to $6M ARR

Marie Martens and Filip Minev set out to be digital nomads. Covid killed that plan in eight days — and the fallback tool they built instead, a form builder that gives almost everything away for free, now makes $6 million a year with a team of ten and zero outside capital.

A travel startup that Covid killed in eight days

In late 2019, Marie Martens and her partner Filip Minev quit their jobs and left Belgium to chase a digital-nomad dream. The plan was Hotspot, a marketplace connecting hotels with travel influencers. Filip, a software engineer who had previously founded Delta — a crypto portfolio app later acquired by eToro — brought the technical chops; Marie, with a decade in B2B marketing, brought the go-to-market brain.

They got as far as Bangkok. Then Covid shut the world down, their customers vanished, and eight days later they flew home. Hotspot was dead. What survived was a habit: while building Hotspot they kept needing forms, and kept hating every form tool they tried. In the summer of 2020 they decided to build the one they wanted — a form builder that works like a document, where you just start typing and insert question blocks. The MVP launched in September 2020; the official Product Hunt launch followed in March 2021 and doubled the user base in a single day, from about 1,500 to 3,000 users.

Free first, on purpose

Tally's free plan has always been the point, not a funnel trick. Unlimited forms, unlimited submissions, forever free — the only price of free is a small “Made with Tally” badge on the form. Everything competitors meter, Tally gives away. There is a single paid plan, Tally Pro at $29 a month, which removes the badge and unlocks the feature stack. No tiers, no aggressive upsells, no sales team, no paid ads.

The math looks impossible until you see the loop: every free form published carries Tally to the people who fill it in. About 2% of users ever pay, but with millions of users that is roughly 17,000 paying customers. Marie has said publicly that without the free plan they would not have grown at this pace. It took a full year to reach $5,000 in monthly recurring revenue — 11,000 users by October 2021 — and another year to reach $10,000 a month. The milestone she remembers most is that $10K: enough, she said, to beat what they would have earned as consultants. They celebrated with a very fancy dinner.

Building in public, one milestone at a time

Marie wrote detailed milestone posts on the Tally blog for years, and the curve they trace is unusually public: $30K MRR by October 2022, $60K in 2023, $100K by February 2024, $150K later that year, then $2M ARR in early 2025, $3M, $4M in October 2025, $5M in April 2026, and $6M ARR — $501K MRR, 2.5 million users — in September 2026. All of it bootstrapped, as a Belgian company, customer-funded from day one.

For the first three years Marie and Filip handled almost everything themselves, including support in an open Slack channel, often replying within the hour while they were awake. They paid themselves minimum salaries for years and only moved to normal ones once they had children. They answered every support message personally for three years before hiring — a pace they later said they had to stop for their own mental health. The team is still intentionally small, around ten people, and the founders have said they could double revenue without hiring much more.

The AI search shift

The most interesting turn in Tally's story is how the growth engine changed without the founders changing strategy. For five years the viral badge loop and word of mouth did the work. Then in 2025–26, AI assistants took over: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews and Perplexity became the number one source of new signups. By September 2026, 43% of new users discovered Tally through an AI assistant. The $2M to $3M jump took four months in mid-2025; the $5M to $6M step followed the same AI-driven wave.

Marie has been candid that the company is not chasing the curve for its own sake. In January 2026 she wrote that Tally was optimizing for quality, not revenue — no new revenue target, just a better product. That stance was tested in August 2026, when a third-party analytics service Tally used was compromised in a data breach. The company disclosed it and kept going; a month later came the $6M milestone post, “6 years in, $6 million far.”

Staying small on purpose

Tally is a reminder that the indie playbook — free-first, build in public, answer your own support, never raise — still works at real scale. Six years in, two founders who set out to travel the world run a $6M ARR business from Belgium with ten people, two young children, and no investors to report to. Marie's stated ambition is not an exit or a bigger round: she wants Tally to be the best and most fun form builder in the world, and she does not think staying small conflicts with that.

The lesson for founders is not “give everything away.” It is that a free plan only works when the free users are the distribution. Tally's free forms advertise Tally on every submission page, to exactly the people who build forms. The product is the marketing, the users are the channel, and the revenue — $6M of it — is what is left over when you refuse to pay for growth you can earn instead.

The playbook

  1. Make the free plan the distribution — Unlimited forms and submissions on free, with a “Made with Tally” badge on every form — free users carry the product to the next user.
  2. One paid plan, no games — A single $29/month Pro plan instead of tier mazes and aggressive upsells; about 2% convert, and that is enough at 2.5 million users.
  3. Build in public with real numbers — Years of milestone posts on the company blog — $5K MRR to $6M ARR, dated and public — turned transparency into trust and signups.
  4. Answer your own support until it hurts — Three years of personally answering every message in an open Slack channel built the loyalty that paid for the next five years of growth.
  5. Ride the channel shift, don't chase it — When AI search became the top acquisition channel in 2025–26, Tally did not change strategy — the product was already the kind of thing assistants recommend.
