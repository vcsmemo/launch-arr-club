The data scientist who proved Google favors big brands — then built the tool for the little guys

30,000 websites, one uncomfortable finding

In 2019, Matt Bentley did what a data scientist does: he measured. Analyzing search rankings across 30,000 websites, he went looking for the shape of competition in Google's results — and found a pattern that Inc.com reported that year. Google's search results carried a built-in bias toward large businesses, and it was big enough to see in the data.

Bentley's finding, as Inc.com's Minda Zetlin wrote: Wikipedia, Amazon, Google, or WebMD very frequently appear in the top three results, while small businesses' websites don't come up until the second or third page. Read that again. Not the fourth result, not the bottom of page one — the second or third page, the part of Google that might as well not exist. For a small business, the takeaway was bleak: you can do everything right and still lose to a logo.

Bentley attributed the pattern to Google's AI having 'programmed in' the human bias toward recognizable brands. Whether you buy the mechanism or not, the measurement stood: 30,000 websites, one uncomfortable truth. The playing field wasn't level, and now there was a number on how unlevel it was.

Part data scientist, part growth marketer

Bentley's background is the product's DNA. His author bio on the CanIRank company blog describes him as 'part data scientist and part growth marketer' — the Founder and Chief Scientist of CanIRank.com, and of GrowthAI.com. That combination — rigorous measurement plus a marketer's instinct for what to do with the numbers — runs through everything CanIRank builds.

An ideamensch founder interview with Bentley covers the same founder story from the operator's side: a data-first person who turned measurement into product. The bias study wasn't a marketing stunt bolted onto a SaaS launch; it was the research that made the tool necessary. First he proved the problem was real. Then he built the answer.

That sequence matters more than it looks. Most SEO tools start from a feature and work backward to a story. Bentley started from a dataset and worked forward to a product — the tool exists because the measurement said it should.

Don't fight battles you can't win

CanIRank's answer to the big-brand bias is pragmatic rather than quixotic. Instead of promising to outrank Amazon — the kind of promise that makes SEO tool marketing embarrassing — the tool analyzes 200+ ranking factors per keyword and gives actionable recommendations, telling small businesses which keyword battles they can actually win and what to do to win them.

The name is the thesis: Can I Rank? For a small business staring at a SERP dominated by Wikipedia and WebMD, an honest answer is more valuable than a motivational one. The tool's job is triage — find the gaps the giants left open, the keywords where a focused small site can still break through, and skip the fights that are unwinnable.

Two hundred ranking factors per keyword is the data-scientist answer to a marketer's question. It's the same instinct that drove the 30,000-website study: measure enough, and the strategy reveals itself. The recommendations are actionable precisely because the analysis is deep — not 'create great content' but the specific factors that move the needle for that keyword.

Free to start, DIY to scale

CanIRank's pricing mirrors the audience: a free $0 tier, with DIY plans starting at $49 per month. The positioning is for the small business owner doing SEO themselves — the same people Bentley's research showed were losing to brands on page one. The free tier is the on-ramp; the DIY tier is the working tool.

No revenue figure has been publicly disclosed for CanIRank, and this story discloses none. The metric that matters here is the research: 30,000 websites analyzed, 200+ factors per keyword, and one uncomfortable truth about how search actually works. A tool company that leads with measurement doesn't need a revenue headline to be credible.

The free tier also functions as the research pipeline. Every small business that asks 'can I rank?' and gets an honest, data-backed answer becomes evidence that the tool works — and the kind of user who tells other small business owners about it.

Measurement as marketing

Bentley's 2019 study did double duty: it was genuine research and the perfect origin story. A founder who quantified the exact problem his tool solves has a credibility most SEO vendors can only buy — and the Inc.com coverage gave that credibility a mainstream audience years before the product story needed it.

CanIRank's bet is that small businesses don't need another tool that says 'create great content and hope.' They need a data scientist's answer to the only question that matters: given the bias, where can I actually win? Bentley measured the bias first — 30,000 websites, top-three results owned by the giants, small businesses buried on page two and three. Then he built the map around it.

The honest answer, it turns out, is a product. CanIRank doesn't promise to fix Google's bias. It promises to tell you where the bias doesn't apply — and that's worth more than any ranking guarantee.
