He Joined an Alpha With No Business Model and Built a Distribution Engine From Free Tools

Ivan Palii joined Sitechecker as Head of Product in 2017 and found an alpha with no traffic, no marketing, and no business model. Five years later it had 350k visitors, 90,000 Chrome extension installs, and 4.5+ ratings everywhere.

A Note on Who's Who

Let's be precise, because the internet rarely is: Ivan Palii is not Sitechecker's founder. He joined the company as Head of Product in 2017, and the founder is not publicly named. What Palii is, is the growth operator who took the product from nothing to something — and his account of how, given to SEO Buddy in August 2022, is one of the most practical SaaS growth stories you'll read.

When he joined, Sitechecker was, in his words, "only an alpha version of the product. It had very little traffic and backlinks, no marketing, no business model." No growth engine, no positioning, no revenue logic. Just an alpha and a hope. Five years later, the numbers told a different story.

Five Years, Built From Zero

Palii's five-year tally, as reported in the SEO Buddy interview: 350,000 unique visitors, 90,000 installs of the Sitechecker Chrome extension, and 4.5+ ratings on G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot. The product claims 85,000+ SEO professionals use it today — an all-in-one platform covering site audits, rank tracking, backlinks, and Google Search Console/GA4 integration, with a Basic plan at $99 a month.

None of that was inevitable. In 2017 Sitechecker was competing against Semrush and Ahrefs, two of the best-funded, best-known brands in SEO. Palii's insight was that the giants' strength was also their weakness: their tools were too complex and too expensive for huge swaths of the market. Sitechecker didn't need to beat Semrush. It needed to serve everyone Semrush was too much for.

The Free-Tool Distribution Engine

The centerpiece of Palii's strategy was free tools as distribution. The team built long-tail landing pages around individual functions of the Site Audit tool — Broken Link Checker, H1 Tag Checker, and similar single-purpose utilities. Each page ranked for its own narrow query and funneled users toward the platform.

The masterstroke was the Chrome extension. It ranked in the top 1–3 on Google for "website checker" keywords — a free product sitting directly on the highest-intent search results in the category. Ninety thousand installs later, the extension was doing the marketing that the company couldn't afford to buy.

It's also worth noting what this approach costs: patience. Free-tool distribution compounds slowly; a Broken Link Checker landing page doesn't rank overnight, and a Chrome extension doesn't reach 90,000 installs in a quarter. Palii's five-year horizon — alpha to 350,000 visitors — is the timeframe this playbook requires. The review-site hustle follows the same logic: G2 and Capterra listings accrete credibility one verified review at a time. None of it is dramatic, and all of it is defensible. When Sitechecker later added rank tracking, backlink monitoring, and GSC/GA4 integration to become a full platform, the distribution was already in place — the new features simply gave 90,000 extension users and a river of free-tool visitors more reasons to convert. The engine Palii built in the free era carried the paid one.

The Launch and Review Hustle

Alongside the free tools, Palii ran the unglamorous registration circuit: BetaList, G2, Capterra, Trustpilot. Getting listed, getting reviewed, getting rated — the kind of work that feels beneath a product team and turns out to be load-bearing for a small SaaS. Those 4.5+ ratings across three review platforms became trust infrastructure: when a small business owner compared Sitechecker to the giants, the ratings said "real company, real users."

It worked because the positioning was honest. Sitechecker didn't claim to be more powerful than Ahrefs. It claimed to be the tool for people who didn't need Ahrefs — and then proved it with free utilities those people could try in thirty seconds.

Two Audiences, One Mission

Palii described two core audiences. The first: small business owners for whom Semrush and Ahrefs were too complex and too expensive. The second: agencies managing many clients, who needed affordable per-site tooling rather than enterprise seats. Both were underserved by the incumbents' pricing and complexity — and both could be reached through free tools and review-site trust.

The mission he articulated is disarmingly simple: "help website owners to get to the top of Google without being or having to hire an SEO expert." No jargon about democratization. Just: you shouldn't need an expert to do the basics. That sentence explains every product decision, from the free checkers to the Chrome extension to the $99 entry price.

The Operator Beyond the Product

Palii didn't stop at Sitechecker. According to a summary of his appearance on The Edward Show (verified via Listen Notes — attribute as such), he runs the SEO newsletter hackthealgo.com, the r/seogaps community, and publishes Looker Studio templates. It's the same playbook, extended: free value, public, compounding.

The arc is a case study in what a growth operator actually does. No funding announcement, no viral moment, no rebrand. Just five years of registering on review sites, building free tools that rank, shipping a Chrome extension to the top of "website checker" results, and aiming the whole machine at the customers the giants priced out. The alpha had no business model. Palii built the business model out of distribution.

The playbook

  1. Register everywhere that matters — BetaList, G2, Capterra, Trustpilot — the launch-and-review circuit Palii ran built trust infrastructure a small SaaS can't buy any other way.
  2. Turn features into free micro-tools — Broken Link Checker, H1 Tag Checker — each audit function became its own long-tail landing page ranking for its own query.
  3. Make the extension the billboard — A free Chrome extension ranking top 1–3 for "website checker" keywords put the product on the highest-intent SERPs in the category.
  4. Serve the customers incumbents price out — Small businesses found Semrush/Ahrefs too complex and expensive; agencies needed affordable multi-client tooling. Sitechecker aimed at both.
  5. State the mission in one sentence — "Help website owners get to the top of Google without being or having to hire an SEO expert" — every product and pricing decision traces to it.
