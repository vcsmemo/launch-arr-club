Today SalesRobot does $70K+ months with zero outside funding.

Laid off, then validated before building

Saurav Gupta lost his startup job during COVID. Like many founders, he had an idea — a LinkedIn lead-generation tool. Unlike most, he made a deliberate decision not to build it yet. As he later explained in an interview with ebizfacts: he wanted to find customers first, before writing a single line of code.

So instead of opening an IDE, he opened Facebook. He found a group full of frustrated users of Dux Soup, the incumbent LinkedIn automation tool — people complaining that it was buggy and kept getting their LinkedIn accounts banned. Gupta started DMing around 40 of those users a day. No pitch. Just one question: had they started using Dux Soup for LinkedIn lead generation yet?

One reply came at 3 AM. A Manchester agency owner running outreach for 60 to 80 clients, paying Dux Soup roughly $4,000 a month — and still getting accounts banned. His message to Gupta was blunt: if you can build something better, I'll happily pay you the same amount.

They co-built version one together. Three months later, that agency owner became SalesRobot's first paying customer at $3,200 a month — before the product was even finished. Gupta had revenue before he had a finished product, which is the exact inversion of how most startups operate.

The safety bet nobody else made

SalesRobot was founded in May 2020 by Gupta and Arun Singh in Delhi, India. From day one, the architecture bet was different from every competitor: instead of browser-extension emulation or datacenter proxies — the techniques that kept getting users banned — SalesRobot was built mobile API-first, using LinkedIn's own mobile app API on the backend with dedicated, local residential IP rotation per account.

The company claims a LinkedIn account ban risk of 0.00001%, a figure no competing tool has publicly matched. Whether or not you believe the exact number, the positioning worked: in a market where every tool promised automation, SalesRobot promised automation that wouldn't get you fired. It runs 24/7 in the cloud with no Chrome extension required, even when your laptop is closed.

That safety-first positioning unlocked the agency market. SalesRobot now serves over 4,100 B2B sales teams and lead generation agencies across 45+ countries, with 28 employees and no external funding. Growth came entirely from word-of-mouth and a white-label agency network of 110+ resellers, some generating $10,000+ per month in recurring profit each. The resellers aren't just a channel — they're proof the product works, because agencies stake their client relationships on it.

AI features that agencies actually pay for

SalesRobot didn't stop at safe automation. The 2025–2026 product cycle added AI capabilities that competitors in its price range don't offer: native AI-personalized voice notes and video messages on LinkedIn — not a third-party integration, but built-in features that clone your voice and face to send personalized audio and video messages to every prospect automatically. Multiple agency customers have published 40%+ reply rates from these campaigns.

The AI Appointment Setter add-on monitors every active campaign around the clock, replies to incoming LinkedIn messages within five minutes — the critical conversion window — and nurtures each lead conversationally until they're ready to book, entirely without human involvement. For agencies managing dozens of client accounts, this is the difference between automation that creates work and automation that removes it.

The company also publishes free playbooks — the LinkedIn Outreach Bible, the 995 Playbook — that function as both content marketing and product education. One playbook details how Gupta poached 8,000+ webinar attendees using LinkedIn events, a strategy competitors don't talk about. The content isn't thought leadership fluff; it's the actual tactics the founder uses, which builds the kind of trust that paid ads can't buy.

What's next: the agency OS

SalesRobot is evolving from a LinkedIn automation tool into something closer to an operating system for outreach agencies. The white-label network, the AI appointment setter, the multi-account dashboard with health monitoring — each piece reduces the operational burden of running outreach at scale.

The trajectory from a COVID layoff to $70K months holds a simple lesson: Gupta never built in a vacuum. His first customer co-designed the product. His resellers distribute it. His playbooks teach the tactics. Every growth lever is a relationship, not a feature — and relationships compound in ways features don't.

The playbook

  1. Sell before you build — Gupta DMed 40 frustrated competitor users a day and landed a $3,200/mo customer before the product was finished. Revenue is the only validation that matters.
  2. Go where users vent — He found his first customers in a Facebook group of angry Dux Soup users. Complaints are pre-qualified demand.
  3. Co-build with your best customer — The Manchester agency owner shaped v1 around real agency workflows, not imagined ones.
  4. Differentiate on the fear, not the feature — Everyone promised automation; SalesRobot promised you wouldn't get banned. Safety was the wedge.
  5. Turn customers into a channel — 110+ white-label resellers distribute the product because agencies stake their reputations on it.
