How QR Code AI hit $16K MRR: the French indie studio behind the viral QR codes

Thibaut Melen and Nicolas Cella run SuperNovae, a tiny French indie studio that ships open-source side projects. One of them — AI-generated QR codes — now does $16,000 a month in verified revenue.

By Thibaut Melen, Nicolas Cella · 2026-10-14 · View on MilestoneWins →

A studio, not a startup

QR Code AI doesn't look like a typical startup success story, because it isn't one. There was no pitch deck, no seed round, no blitzscaling. There is SuperNovae — a small French indie studio run by Thibaut Melen and Nicolas Cella — and a habit of building things in the open.

SuperNovae operates as a studio rather than a single-product company. The founders ship open-source projects, experiment with new technology, and let the market decide what's worth keeping. QR Code AI started as one of those experiments: what happens when you point generative AI at the humble QR code?

The answer turned out to be surprisingly lucrative. AI-generated QR codes — scannable codes blended into artistic images — caught a wave of attention from marketers and brands looking for something more distinctive than black-and-white squares. What began as a technical curiosity became a product with real, paying customers.

The revenue is verified, not claimed. QR Code AI's Stripe data is public through TrustMRR, showing $16,000 in monthly recurring revenue and $304,000 in total revenue. In an era of inflated startup metrics, that kind of transparency is itself a statement about how the studio operates.

Riding the generative AI wave sideways

The timing mattered enormously. QR Code AI launched into the slipstream of the generative image boom — the months when every new Stable Diffusion trick went viral on social media. AI QR codes were one of those tricks: functional art that spread because people shared the images themselves.

But virality alone doesn't make a business. Plenty of AI demos went viral in 2023 and earned their creators nothing. What separated QR Code AI was the studio's instinct to productize quickly — to turn the demo into something a marketing team could actually buy, with accounts, billing, and an API, before the novelty faded.

The product solved a real, if narrow, problem. Brands spend heavily on visual identity, and standard QR codes are ugly. An AI-generated code that actually looks good — that matches a campaign's aesthetic while still scanning reliably — is worth paying for if you're running packaging, posters, or event materials at any scale.

This is the indie hacker's version of market timing: you don't need to predict the wave, you need to be building in the water when it arrives. SuperNovae's habit of shipping experiments meant they had something ready when the AI-art moment hit. Most studios would have admired the trend. They monetized it.

The open-source flywheel

SuperNovae's open-source work does quiet marketing for everything the studio ships. Developers who discover the studio through a GitHub repo become aware of the commercial products. The open-source reputation signals technical credibility — important when you're asking marketers to trust an AI tool with their brand assets.

This flywheel is hard to replicate and easy to underestimate. Traditional SaaS companies buy attention with ads or content marketing. An indie studio with genuine open-source output earns attention as a byproduct of doing interesting work. The marketing budget is zero; the marketing is the work itself.

The model also shapes the product. When your users include developers, the product tends to get an API early, documentation that's actually accurate, and pricing that's transparent. QR Code AI's developer-friendly surface area — the kind of thing a studio of builders would naturally include — opened up embedding and white-label use cases that pure consumer tools miss.

There is a broader pattern here worth naming: the most durable indie businesses are built by people who would build the thing anyway. The revenue is a side effect of obsession, not the goal of a spreadsheet. That authenticity is difficult to fake and, as QR Code AI shows, surprisingly monetizable.

$16K MRR on a side project

Sixteen thousand dollars a month won't make headlines next to venture-backed AI startups raising hundreds of millions. But context matters. QR Code AI is reportedly a side project from a two-person studio, with no funding, no sales team, and minimal overhead. On those economics, $16K MRR is not a consolation prize — it's a genuinely great business.

The $304,000 in total verified revenue tells the deeper story: this wasn't a flash in the pan. Customers stayed. The product kept working after the viral moment passed, because it solved a real problem rather than just demonstrating a trick. Retention is the difference between a demo and a business.

For founders watching from the sidelines, QR Code AI offers a template that's more accessible than the unicorn narrative. You don't need to raise capital or hire fifty people. You need a studio habit of shipping, the judgment to productize what resonates, and the patience to let a small product compound.

Thibaut Melen and Nicolas Cella didn't set out to build a QR code empire. They built something interesting, noticed people would pay for it, and kept going. Sometimes that's the whole playbook — and $16,000 a month is a fine reward for it.

The French indie scene has quietly produced a string of these businesses: small teams, global customers, verified revenue, no theater. QR Code AI belongs in that lineage — proof that you can win by being interesting before you try to be profitable.

The playbook

  1. Ship experiments, productize what resonates — SuperNovae's studio model meant QR Code AI already existed as an experiment when the AI-art wave hit — they just had to add billing.
  2. Verify revenue publicly — Publishing Stripe data through TrustMRR builds trust in a market full of inflated claims.
  3. Let open source do the marketing — Genuine open-source output earns developer attention as a byproduct — zero ad spend required.
  4. Build for the wave you're already in — You can't predict virality, but you can be building in the water when it arrives.
  5. Solve the boring version of the cool demo — Viral AI QR art became a business when it got accounts, an API, and reliable scanning for brand use cases.
