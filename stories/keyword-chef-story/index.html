The solopreneur who automated his own keyword research into a full-time business

Ben Adler was a programmer building content sites when he got tired of doing keyword research by hand. He coded his own formulas into Keyword Chef — and turned a personal automation into a full-time, solo SaaS business.

Scratch your own itch — literally

Ben Adler's path to Keyword Chef ran through content sites. He was building niche sites and doing keyword research the manual way — pulling SERP data, eyeballing competition, repeating the same formulas spreadsheet after spreadsheet. It was the kind of repetitive work that content builders accept as the cost of doing business: hours of manual analysis for every batch of topics.

But Adler wasn't just a content builder. He was a programmer. And a programmer looks at a repeated manual process differently — not as drudgery to endure, but as a function to automate. The formulas he was running by hand were deterministic; the judgment calls were pattern-matching. The whole workflow was, in his eyes, code waiting to be written.

So he wrote it. The keyword research formulas became software, the software got a name, and Keyword Chef was founded in 2021 — a long-tail keyword research tool with real SERP competition scoring, built by someone who had done the manual version a thousand times and refused to do it a thousand more.

From builder to solopreneur founder

On The Opportunity podcast (episode 58, October 2021), Adler described the transition from building content sites to creating a SaaS business from scratch as a solopreneur — per the episode summary as verified via Listen Notes. He used his past experience as a programmer to turn his keyword research workflow into a product others could use.

That's the whole origin story in one move: the product is the automation of the founder's own workflow. There's no market research phase to romanticize, no pivot narrative, no 'we talked to a hundred users.' Adler was the first user, the formulas were already proven on his own sites, and the question was simply whether other site builders wanted the shortcut.

Niche Pursuits later profiled the outcome in an interview titled 'How Ben Adler Successfully Bootstrapped Keyword Chef Into a Full-time Business.' The interview confirms what the headline says: Keyword Chef supports Adler full-time. It states no specific revenue number, and this story states none either. The milestone is the livelihood, not the valuation.

What the tool actually finds

Keyword Chef's differentiation is in the SERP analysis. Rather than handing users raw keyword lists — the commodity output of every keyword tool — it scores real competition, analyzing what's actually ranking for a term and how hard it would be to beat. For a content site builder, that's the difference between a list of ideas and a ranked list of opportunities.

The tool is particularly good at surfacing low-competition openings in places traditional tools overlook: forums, UGC content, the long tail where small sites can still rank. That focus matters because it's exactly where a solo site builder competes — not against the giants for head terms, but in the quiet corners of the SERP where a well-targeted article can still win.

The scale of that analysis is the hook of a Niche Pursuits podcast episode on the tool — titled, per its transcript on Glasp, 'His Tool Found 37 Million Keywords...' — covering how Adler chooses the best topics to grow a blog from automated SERP data. Whether you read that number as a milestone or a marketing hook, it captures the product's thesis: automate the analysis at a scale no human can match.

Credit-based, solo, sustainable

Keyword Chef runs on credit-based pricing rather than the flat monthly tiers most SEO tools use. Buy credits, run research, spend what you use. It's a model that fits the audience Adler came from — solo site builders whose research needs spike and dip with their publishing cadence — and it fits the founder too: a tool that sells itself without an enterprise sales motion.

The business model matches the operator. One person, no funding story, no growth-at-all-costs narrative, no disclosed revenue figure to headline. Adler built the tool he needed, found that other site builders needed it too, and bootstrapped it into his full-time living. The company is the size the founder wants it to be.

There's a discipline in that restraint. Credit-based pricing means every credit sold is a unit of work delivered — no vanity subscriber counts, no annual contracts masking churn. The product has to earn its next purchase, one research session at a time.

The quiet thesis

Keyword Chef's story is the solopreneur thesis in its purest form: deep domain experience (content sites) plus a technical skill (programming) plus a repetitive personal pain (manual keyword research) equals a product with built-in product-market fit. Adler didn't need to discover the problem; he had been living inside it for years.

The 2021 founding date puts the company in the cohort of tools that grew up alongside the content-site boom — and the shift toward long-tail, low-competition strategy that defined it. The tool's automated SERP analysis is, in a sense, the industrialization of what the best niche site builders were already doing by hand.

Adler didn't start with a market analysis. He started with his own spreadsheets, automated them, and the full-time business followed. No revenue figure has been publicly disclosed — and for a story about sustainable solo building, the full-time income is the milestone that matters. The chef cooked for himself first; the restaurant came later.

The playbook

  1. Automate your own repetitive workflow first — Adler built Keyword Chef from the keyword research formulas he was already running by hand as a programmer building content sites (The Opportunity podcast, EP 58, via Listen Notes summary).
  2. Ship the painkiller you needed — The product was the automation of his own spreadsheets — built-in product-market fit, because he was the first customer.
  3. Score real competition, not vanity metrics — Keyword Chef's edge is automated SERP analysis finding low-competition openings in forums and UGC content — surfacing opportunities raw keyword lists miss.
  4. Price for your audience — Credit-based pricing fits the solo site-builder audience — pay for research as you use it, not a flat monthly tier.
  5. Bootstrap to full-time, quietly — Niche Pursuits confirms the tool became Adler's full-time business. No funding, no disclosed revenue — the solo sustainability story stands on its own.
