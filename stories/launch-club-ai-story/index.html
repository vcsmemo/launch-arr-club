How Launch Club AI hit $68.7K/30d: the 25-year marketer who sold his agency and bet on Reddit

Ken Savage taught himself HTML from a For Dummies book in the 90s, knocked on industrial-park doors to sell websites, built Product Launch Pro into a six-figure acquisition, then started over with a thesis 25 years in the making: demand already exists — you just have to show up where it's asking. That place is Reddit.

Knocking on doors with HTML For Dummies

Ken Savage was an early adopter before the term existed. He started coding in the 1980s, was one of the first on the internet in the 90s, and taught himself HTML from an HTML For Dummies book — then bought kensavage.com and knocked on doors in industrial parks asking if businesses wanted websites. Most of the time they did. He built 100 websites in a year.

That origin story contains the entire Savage playbook: learn the tool, find where demand already exists, and show up in person. He didn't invent demand for websites; he found businesses that needed them and walked through their doors. Twenty-five years later, Launch Club AI is the same playbook applied to Reddit — except now the "doors" are threads where buyers compare products and ask for recommendations.

In the following decade, Savage moved into SEO and digital marketing, becoming obsessed with growth hacks. In the early 2000s that meant keyword-stuffing articles; as SEO got harder, he learned to create content too interesting to ignore. His specialty became lead magnets with irresistible hooks — one targeting cybersecurity companies featured an incomplete video of a hack in progress, requiring an email to see the rest.

The agency, the exit, and the restart

Savage built Product Launch Pro, an agency specializing in marketing growth hacks. The package was simple: pay $1,000 and he'd expedite your growth in a month. He was so good at it that clients started asking for more months. Eventually he quit his day job because the agency was making more money.

Then he did something most agency owners never do: he decided he wanted a business that didn't require his full-time presence. He listed Product Launch Pro on Acquire and sold it for a six-figure acquisition. In an interview with Acquire's Startup Acquisition Stories, he described the arc from explosive growth hacker to founder seeking leverage — the agency proved the playbook worked, but it didn't scale without him.

After the exit, Savage started building Launch Club — originally conceived as a community-based marketing business where people could join and boost each other's marketing efforts online. Over seven years, the operation helped over 1,100 clients and published more than 4 million pieces of content on Reddit. That's not a typo: four million. The scale of Reddit execution became the moat.

The Reddit thesis

Savage's core insight, refined over two decades, is disarmingly simple: the channels change every few years, but the job stays the same. Find where people are already asking for a solution. Be the most useful answer. Reddit is where that happens now — threads where buyers compare products, ask for recommendations, and trust strangers more than ads.

But 99% of companies don't know where to look or how to use Reddit. So Savage built a scraper that finds the threads where your buyers are talking: input a keyword, subreddit, or competitor name, and get every matching post and comment in one export. No more opening 40 tabs and losing the good one. The writing is the easy part of Reddit, he argues — finding the right threads is the real work, and the tool does the heavy lifting.

Launch Club AI layers AI search on top: it helps brands identify high-value Reddit conversations and turn them into traffic via AI search — because Reddit threads are increasingly what ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews cite. Savage's 25 years of pattern recognition led him to the same conclusion as the AEO crowd, from the opposite direction: he started with the community and discovered the AI angle, rather than starting with AI and discovering the community.

He's also a Search Engine Land author, a 20-year digital marketing mentor, and — when not working — usually found backpacking the White Mountains of New Hampshire or watching hockey. The through-line across all of it: show up where people already are, and be useful.

What's next: from scraper to AI search engine

Launch Club AI sits at the intersection of two massive shifts: Reddit's emergence as the internet's trust layer, and AI assistants citing Reddit as a primary source. The scraper finds the conversations; the AI layer turns them into search visibility. As AI search grows, every brand will need what Launch Club does — the question is whether they build it themselves or buy it from the team with 4 million Reddit posts of experience.

The playbook

  1. Demand already exists — find it — Savage's 25-year thesis: don't create demand, show up where it's already asking. Reddit threads are today's industrial parks.
  2. Sell the outcome, not the hours — The $1,000/month growth package sold results. Clients asked for more months because the first one worked.
  3. Exit what doesn't scale without you — Selling Product Launch Pro for six figures freed Savage to build something with leverage instead of trading time.
  4. Do the unsexy work at insane scale — 4 million Reddit posts isn't a growth hack. It's operational excellence compounded over seven years.
  5. Let the platform shift come to you — Savage started with Reddit community and discovered AI search; the thesis held through the channel change.
