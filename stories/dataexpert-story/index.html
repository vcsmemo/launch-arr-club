How DataExpert hit $63K MRR: the Big Tech engineer who teaches what FAANG won't

Zach Wilson led data engineering teams at Facebook, Netflix, and Airbnb. Then he noticed the knowledge gap: Big Tech hoards its data engineering playbook, and everyone else is guessing. DataExpert.io — launched March 2023, now $63K MRR with a staff of 18 — is his answer: teach data engineering like a Big Tech engineer, to anyone willing to learn.

From FAANG to the classroom

Zach Wilson's resume reads like a data engineering hall of fame: leadership roles at Facebook, Netflix, and Airbnb, working on growth analytics, security infrastructure, and pricing and availability systems. He led teams of data engineers and software engineers at companies where data infrastructure operates at planetary scale.

But the credential that matters most for DataExpert isn't the logos — it's what Wilson saw from inside: the gap between how Big Tech does data engineering and what the rest of the industry knows. The techniques, the architecture patterns, the hard-won lessons about what breaks at scale — all of it locked inside companies that treat engineering knowledge as competitive advantage. Everyone else learns by trial and error, or from outdated tutorials.

Wilson's thesis was simple: that knowledge gap is a business. In March 2023, he launched DataExpert.io, a data engineering academy. The first offering was a live boot camp in Spring 2023 — not a recorded course, but live instruction from someone who'd actually run data infrastructure at Netflix scale. The format mattered: live cohorts create accountability, community, and the kind of peer learning that async video can't replicate.

Daily posts, 1M followers, zero ad spend

DataExpert's growth engine is Wilson himself. He posts daily about data engineering — and about mental health, bringing a holistic approach to professional development that resonates beyond the technical content. He's open about his experiences with ADHD, using personal challenges to connect with and inspire others in the field.

The result: approximately 1 million followers across social platforms, built on consistency and authenticity rather than ad spend. Every post is both content marketing and community building — aspiring data engineers follow for the technical insights and stay for the human ones. When enrollment opens for a bootcamp, the audience is already warm.

The academy has grown to a staff of 18, reflecting the commitment to expanding educational resources. The content archive on DataExpert.io provides ongoing value for learners and professionals, creating an SEO and referral flywheel: free content attracts learners, learners become students, students become advocates.

Wilson's media presence extends to podcasts — including a featured episode on "What's New In Data" where he discussed the journey from Think Big Analytics through FAANG leadership to education, and his take on AI's role in data engineering. His position is nuanced: while LLMs can generate SQL, they haven't replaced the human judgment in stakeholder communication and conceptual data modeling. The human element, he argues, remains the linchpin.

The bootcamp model that works

DataExpert's programs follow a proven structure: live online cohorts, typically 7 weeks, with payment plans and free access tiers. The curriculum covers the full data engineering interview and job spectrum — SQL, data modeling, data architecture, behavioral interviews, product sense — plus emerging topics like LLM-driven data engineering.

The third bootcamp ran November to December 2023, and the academy has continued iterating. The model works because it solves the exact problem Wilson identified: structured, expert-led learning for a field where most people are self-taught from fragments. Students aren't buying videos; they're buying the shortcut through years of trial and error, taught by someone who's already made the mistakes at the highest level.

Social proof compounds: student testimonials, capstone projects (like the data engineering capstones published on GitHub), and career outcomes create the credibility loop that justifies premium pricing in a market flooded with $20 Udemy courses.

What's next: upskilling at scale

Wilson's stated goal is direct: upskill as many data knowledge workers as he can. The $63K MRR is a milestone, not a destination — the addressable market is every data professional who knows they're behind the curve but doesn't know where to start.

The academy model has natural expansion paths: corporate training, advanced specializations, AI-era data engineering tracks. The "What's New In Data" podcast appearance and Striim-published interview show Wilson building authority beyond his own platform — each media hit funnels back to the academy. With 18 staff and growing, DataExpert is transitioning from a founder-led bootcamp into an institution, which is exactly what the data engineering field needs: not another course marketplace, but a school with a point of view.

But the core insight remains unchanged from day one: Big Tech's playbook shouldn't be a secret, and teaching it honestly — with the human stuff included — is a business that compounds.

The playbook

  1. Monetize the knowledge gap — Big Tech hoards data engineering knowledge; Wilson packaged it as an academy. The gap between insider and outsider knowledge is always a business.
  2. Teach live, not just recorded — Live cohorts create accountability and community that async courses can't match, justifying premium pricing.
  3. Build in public, daily — 1M followers from daily posts about data engineering and mental health. Consistency beats campaigns.
  4. Be human, not just expert — Openness about ADHD and mental health differentiates Wilson from every other "FAANG engineer teaches you" brand.
  5. Start with the transformation — Students buy the shortcut through years of trial and error, not videos. Sell the outcome.
