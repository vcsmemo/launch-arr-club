Illness Took Everything. Low-Competition Keywords Gave It Back — Then He Turned the Method Into Software

Brandon Gaille built a multimillion-dollar affiliate company in his 20s, lost it all to a rare disease, rebuilt to millions of visitors with low-competition keywords — and built RankIQ only after half his own students couldn't learn the method by hand.

The First Empire

In his 20s, Brandon Gaille built a multimillion-dollar content and affiliate company. On the MiloTree Blogger Genius Podcast (#238, 2022), he described ranking #1 on Google for terms like "credit report" and "credit cards" — some of the most competitive, most lucrative keywords on the internet. He wasn't a blogger dabbling in SEO. He was operating at the highest level of the affiliate game, in the most cutthroat verticals, and winning.

That matters because everything that follows is filtered through it. Gaille didn't discover SEO as a beginner and get lucky. He mastered it at scale, in money keywords, before most of today's SEO tool founders had registered a domain. The expertise was real — and then, almost all at once, it was gone.

The Fall

Gaille developed dysautonomia, a rare disease that caused cognitive loss. He was pushed out by his partner. Within two years, the wealth went to zero. Read that again: from a multimillion-dollar company ranking #1 for "credit cards" to nothing, in twenty-four months, because his body failed him.

He has spoken about this period with unusual candor across podcasts. On Eat Blog Talk (#300, 2024), he framed the arc in terms of faith: "With God, anything is possible. He took me from having nothing and put me right next to my wife that led to an incredible family. It guided us through my health condition, cancer. He brought me through it to becoming a blogger." The health battles didn't end with dysautonomia — cancer came later too. This is a founder story where the obstacles aren't competitors or funding. They're medical.

The Side Hustle Show put the financial wreckage in numbers: from serious health issues and $500,000 in debt to seven figures a year in ad revenue. The distance between those two points is the story.

The Rebuild, Keyword by Keyword

After sleep-treatment recovery, Gaille started a blog in April 2013. He had one strategy, and it was the opposite of his 20s: instead of fighting for "credit cards," he targeted low-competition keywords — terms nobody was fighting over but people were still searching. Sixty percent of his articles targeted low-competition keywords, he told MiloTree.

The results were startling. One hundred thousand monthly visitors in four months. Over a million after a year. The host of Eat Blog Talk cited the mature numbers: 5 million monthly visitors and 100,000+ Google #1 rankings (host-cited data — attribute as such). The Side Hustle Show cited three sites with 6 million monthly visitors combined. Whatever the exact peak, the mechanism was the same: find the keywords the giants ignore, rank fast, compound.

It's a strategy with a particular elegance for someone rebuilding from zero. You don't need authority to rank for low-competition terms. You need the discipline to find them and the patience to publish. Gaille had both — and a decade of ranking intuition that told him which "easy" keywords were actually worth targeting.

The Course That Half the Students Couldn't Learn

Success brought an audience. Gaille launched a podcast — "The Blogging Millionaire," which went on to teach 20,000+ bloggers — and a $499 SEO course teaching the low-competition method. And then he hit a wall that had nothing to do with Google.

"Fifty percent of the people taking my course telling me that they just can't figure out how to do this," he told MiloTree. "They don't understand how to work the tool or to do the calculations." He spent two years simplifying the course — two full years of making the method easier, clearer, more step-by-step. And still, half the students couldn't replicate it by hand.

Most educators would have concluded the students weren't trying hard enough. Gaille concluded the method was too hard to do manually. That's a founder's read: the problem isn't the user, it's the interface. If half your paying students can't do the calculations after two years of simplification, the answer isn't a better course. It's software that does the calculations for them.

RankIQ: The Method, Automated

So he built the calculations into software. That software became RankIQ: AI SEO reports plus a keyword library for bloggers, starting at $49 a month, that grades your draft from A++ to D and tells you exactly what to fix. The $499 course's hard parts — the keyword math, the competitive analysis, the optimization checklist — became a product.

The market validated the bet. RankIQ ranked #1 in customer satisfaction and ease of use among 333 SEO tools on G2 — the exact two dimensions you'd expect from a tool born out of "my students can't do this by hand." It was designed, from the first line of code, to be usable by the 50% who couldn't learn the manual method.

The full arc: multimillion-dollar affiliate company in his 20s. Dysautonomia, a partner's betrayal, wealth to zero in two years, $500,000 in debt. A blog in April 2013, 60% low-competition keywords, 100k visitors in four months, a million in a year. A course half the students couldn't learn. And finally, software that made the method automatic. RankIQ isn't a tool built from a market analysis. It's a tool built from a hospital bed, a comeback, and two years of watching good students fail at something that shouldn't have been that hard.

The playbook

  1. Target the keywords giants ignore — 60% of articles aimed at low-competition keywords — you don't need authority to rank where nobody is fighting.
  2. Rebuild with what you know, not what you had — After losing everything, Gaille didn't chase "credit cards" again; he applied a decade of ranking intuition to winnable terms.
  3. Simplify for two years before you automate — He spent two years simplifying the $499 course before concluding the manual method itself was the problem.
  4. Build software when teaching fails at scale — 50% of students couldn't do the calculations by hand — so he built the calculations into RankIQ instead of blaming the students.
  5. Grade the work, don't just describe it — RankIQ's A++-to-D draft grading turns abstract SEO advice into a score the struggling 50% can act on.
