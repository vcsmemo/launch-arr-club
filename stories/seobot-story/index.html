How SEOBOT hit $47K MRR: the village kid who built 20 products to $2M ARR

John Rush was born in a Turkish ethnic-minority village of 500 people with no electricity until he was 8. His father was the village inventor. Twenty years later, Rush runs 20+ products at $2M ARR — including SEOBOT, the fully autonomous SEO robot that got 4,000 signups at launch. His rule: only build what he'd use himself.

No electricity until age 8

You might assume someone running 20+ products at $2M ARR came from privilege. John Rush's story starts in the opposite place: a small ethnic-minority village of about 500 people in a remote part of Turkey. The village didn't have proper electricity — he first learned what electricity was at age 8. His father, fortunately, was an inventor in the village, and that inventive streak seems to have been the inheritance that mattered.

Rush has described his journey as a 20-year overnight success. The early years were suffering and experimentation — the kind of grinding that doesn't make for good Twitter threads but builds the instincts that later look like genius. By the time he started shipping products in volume, he had a clear philosophy forged from failures: most ideas don't need investor money, most products shouldn't chase unicorn status, and the goal is profitable businesses that do one thing really well for one niche.

That philosophy became MarsX and the SaaS OS vision: a portfolio of tools for solo makers and small bootstrapped teams, each the best in its niche, most never exceeding $1M ARR — and that's fine. It's a deliberate rejection of the venture-scale-or-die model, and the $2M combined ARR suggests the math works.

The 13-step playbook

Rush published his exact method — a 13-step journey from idea to revenue that he's used for every startup he's launched. Some were hits, others failures, but the process is the constant. Step one is the filter: he only builds products he would use himself. Every time he strayed from that rule, it didn't end well.

Then comes the research phase: hours in Google Search Console digging into keyword traffic trends. Once a promising topic catches his eye, he forms a niche idea around it — then shares it publicly through tweets, blog posts, even discussions with his wife (whose boredom, he jokes, is a reliable indicator of whether to proceed).

If there's traction, he sets up a landing page with a waitlist using Unicorn Platform, finds online conversations where people express the pain point, and directs them to the waitlist. He lists every project on 100+ directories via ListingBott for immediate traffic, turns on SEOBOT for blog optimization — then deliberately steps back for a month.

The validation criteria before writing code are specific: at least 100 people on the waitlist, 1,000+ monthly visitors, minimum 300 organic visitors. Only then does he build an MVP — fast, either self-coded or with a partner — while staying in touch with waitlisters for feedback before they even try the product. Beta testers get early access; their feedback shapes features over weeks and months.

SEOBOT: the product that eats its own dogfood

SEOBOT is the purest expression of Rush's philosophy: a fully autonomous SEO robot with AI agents for busy founders. It's the tool he uses to grow his own portfolio — every product launch gets listed on 100+ directories and gets SEOBOT-powered blog optimization. The product is the process, productized.

At launch, SEOBOT got 4,000 users signing up. People just loved it, Rush wrote — the rare launch where traction was immediate rather than grinded out. The product resonated because it solved the exact problem every indie hacker faces: SEO is essential but time-consuming, and most founders would rather be building.

Rush's broader 2024 plan was characteristically ambitious: launching 50 SaaS tools for founders, investing all personal money and profits into building the best tools for solo makers. The dream, as he put it: a world where indie makers and bootstrapped teams take over from corporations. SEOBOT at $47K MRR is one piece of that portfolio — significant on its own, but more interesting as part of the machine.

His earlier dev.to post about nearly being killed by Series A funding adds context: after cutting all sales and marketing, going product-led, and rebuilding around 100% inbound (SEO via SEOBOT plus affiliate partners at 33% revenue share), the business went breakeven in three months. The lesson stuck: don't build unicorns, build profitable niche leaders.

What's next: 50 tools and the SaaS OS

Rush is going all in on the portfolio model — dozens of tools, each serving a specific niche, cross-promoted through shared infrastructure (directories, SEO, affiliates). SEOBOT is both a product and the growth engine for everything else. The $2M ARR is the current total; the system is designed to compound.

From a village with no electricity to a one-man software conglomerate — the through-line isn't talent or luck, but a process refined over 20 years and the discipline to follow it every single time.

The playbook

  1. Only build what you'd use — Rush's rule #1. Every deviation ended badly. SEOBOT exists because he needed it for his own launches.
  2. Validate with numbers, not opinions — 100 waitlist signups, 1,000 monthly visitors, 300 organic. No code until the thresholds hit.
  3. Step back for a month — After the landing page goes live, wait. Let organic interest prove itself before investing build time.
  4. List everywhere, immediately — 100+ directories via ListingBott for instant traffic. Distribution before product.
  5. Build a portfolio, not a unicorn — 20+ products at $2M combined ARR beats one moonshot. Niche leadership compounds.
