The Agency Tool That Outranked Marks & Spencer — Then Became the Business

Andy Chadwick and Suganthan Mohanadasan built Snippet Digital’s client-winning methodology into Keyword Insights, an AI-driven content intelligence platform behind a furniture startup’s run from zero to 80,000 monthly visits — past M&S, Next and Oak Furnitureland.

Self-taught SEO, from a roller shutter business

Andy Chadwick’s path into SEO did not run through a marketing degree or a graduate scheme. Speaking on the Advanced Web Ranking Search Session, he described himself as Scottish, living in England, who taught himself SEO in his early twenties — while running a roller shutter business. It is the classic practitioner origin: learn the craft because your own livelihood depends on it.

That self-taught foundation turned into agency work. On The SEO Show episode 43, Chadwick described himself as one of the co-founders of Snippet Consulting and the founder of Snippet Digital, a UK SEO agency. On the AWR session he added that he had worked with his co-founder Suganthan Mohanadasan — “another big name,” in the host’s framing — for five years. The partnership, not just the individual, is the engine of the story: two operators who spent half a decade refining a methodology together before it ever became software.

That origin shows up in how Chadwick talks about the work. He did not learn SEO to sell SEO; he learned it to keep his own business visible, and the agency years that followed were a continuation of the same discipline — testing on real businesses, where rankings move revenue. Keyword Insights inherited that practitioner’s stance: every feature descends from something that had to work on a client’s site, not a whiteboard.

From agency methodology to SaaS

The pivot from services to software is the hinge of the Keyword Insights story. On The SEO Show, Chadwick put it plainly: the agency had “ended up spinning one, well, our most famous one called Keyword Insights, which is a SaaS tool which helps basically turbocharge your content strategy.” The tool was not invented to fill a market gap identified in a spreadsheet; it was the internal machinery of a working agency, productized.

What the platform does reflects that origin. Chadwick describes Keyword Insights as an AI-driven content intelligence platform that uses AI to cluster keywords, uncover search intent, and build topical authority at scale. The product today offers keyword clustering by SERP intent for up to 2.5 million keywords, plus content briefs and AI content generation, starting at $58 per month — the agency’s content playbook, packaged as software.

That spin-out logic is the whole thesis of agency-to-SaaS done right: the two agencies — Snippet Consulting and Snippet Digital — were the laboratory, and of everything the agency built, Keyword Insights was, in Chadwick’s own words, “our most famous one.” The famous one became the business.

The receipts: a furniture startup versus the high street

Agency tools live or die on results, and the case studies Chadwick cites are strikingly concrete. On The SEO Show, he described a UK furniture startup that went from nothing to 80,000 visits per month within a year — outranking Marks & Spencer, Next, and Oak Furnitureland along the way. For a startup to overtake three of Britain’s most established retail and furniture brands in organic search within twelve months is the kind of result that sells methodology better than any pitch.

He paired it with a US client story: traffic growing from 500,000 to 600–700,000 visits — roughly a 110% increase — in just three to four months. These are not vanity metrics from a slide deck; they are the client outcomes of the exact methodology the SaaS encodes. The product’s credibility rests on the agency’s track record, which is precisely why spinning it out of Snippet Digital worked.

Both numbers come straight from Chadwick’s own mouth on The SEO Show episode 43 — a verified transcript, not a marketing page. That matters because it is the founder, in public, attaching his name to the outcomes. For a SaaS that claims to turbocharge content strategy, the pitch is not the algorithm; it is the furniture startup past M&S and the American client doubled in a quarter.

Naming the concepts: ambient visibility and 3D search intent

Chadwick has also done the conceptual work that separates a tool from a movement. In the AWR conversation he is credited with coining “ambient visibility” and “3D search intent,” framing topical authority for multi-platform search — the idea that authority now has to be built across search surfaces, not just classic Google results.

This matters commercially, not just intellectually. Naming a concept gives the methodology a handle that the market can repeat, and every repetition markets the tool that operationalizes it. Keyword Insights does not just cluster keywords; it sells a named way of thinking about authority — topical authority at scale — that the founders can teach, speak about, and attach to the product.

The “3D” framing is doing specific work. Search intent has a depth beyond the old informational-versus-transactional split, and Chadwick’s language argues that multi-platform search — visibility across the surfaces where people now look for things — demands authority built in dimensions, not lines. The coined terms are shorthand for a bigger thesis: the agency’s methodology was never about gaming one results page, but about becoming the obvious answer wherever the question gets asked.

The American client and the five-year partnership

The furniture startup was not the only receipt. On The SEO Show, Chadwick described a US client that grew from 500,000 to between 600,000 and 700,000 visits — a gain of roughly 110% — in just three to four months using the same topical-authority methodology. Two case studies, two continents, the same playbook: cluster keywords by intent, build hub-and-spoke content, measure topical authority rather than individual keyword positions.

The partnership behind the tool matters too. Chadwick told Advanced Web Ranking that he had worked with Suganthan Mohanadasan for five years before Keyword Insights became a business — five years of shared client work is the unglamorous foundation most overnight SaaS successes are actually built on. The tool also looks beyond Google: it surfaces Reddit, Quora, and YouTube opportunities, aimed at the search surfaces where buying decisions increasingly start.

What the Keyword Insights story teaches

The Keyword Insights arc is the purest version of the agency-to-SaaS playbook: spend five years winning clients with a methodology, then productize the machinery. The risk of that path is that the tool ends up as a side project; the reason it worked here is that the methodology had receipts — a furniture startup past M&S, a US client up 110% — that made the software credible on day one.

There is a second lesson in the conceptual layer. By naming “ambient visibility” and “3D search intent,” the founders gave the market language for the problem their tool solves, which is a force multiplier no feature list can match. And by building for scale — clustering up to 2.5 million keywords — they ensured the product could serve operations far larger than the agency that birthed it. The internal tool outgrew its maker, which is exactly what was supposed to happen.

Finally, the story is a reminder that the best agency-to-SaaS spinouts are never really a leap — they are an unveiling. Snippet Digital and Snippet Consulting had already done the hard part: five years of Chadwick and Mohanadasan working together, real clients, real rankings, real visits per month. Keyword Insights just took the machinery that produced those results and made it buyable. The customers were always the proof; the software is the distribution.

The playbook

  1. Productize what already wins clients — Keyword Insights was Snippet Digital’s internal content machinery before it was a SaaS — the methodology was proven on paying clients first.
  2. Lead with named, checkable wins — A furniture startup from zero to 80,000 monthly visits past M&S, Next and Oak Furnitureland — specific outcomes make the software credible instantly.
  3. Name the concept, not just the feature — Coined terms like “ambient visibility” and “3D search intent” give the market language for the problem — and every mention markets the tool.
  4. Build for operations bigger than your own — Clustering up to 2.5 million keywords means the product serves content operations far larger than the agency that created it.
  5. Keep the practitioner partnership at the core — Five years of Chadwick and Mohanadasan refining the methodology together before productizing it — depth of partnership as product depth.
