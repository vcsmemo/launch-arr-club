How Yorby hit $9.4k MRR: an ex-banker and an ex-Googler bet on AI-generated content

Andrew Meng and Thomas Kim are building Yorby AI, an AI-powered content and UGC platform. Meng — who appeared on the Frankly Speaking podcast to talk about AI and content — came from banking, Kim from Google. The company is already at $9.4k MRR.

The banker and the Googler

Andrew Meng doesn't look like a typical AI founder. Before starting Yorby AI, he worked in banking — an industry not exactly known for producing startup founders. His co-founder, Thomas Kim, came from Google. Together, they're building Yorby, an AI platform focused on content and user-generated content (UGC), which has already reached $9.4k in monthly recurring revenue.

The pairing is interesting precisely because it's unusual. The AI content space is crowded with technical founders racing to build slightly better text generators. Meng brings something different: an operator's understanding of what businesses actually pay for. When he appeared on the Frankly Speaking podcast — hosted by Frank Niu — to discuss the future of content and AI UGC, he wasn't pitching features. He was laying out a thesis about where content marketing is going and why AI-generated content, done right, becomes a business asset rather than a spam machine.

Yorby's bet is that UGC — the authentic, creator-style content that outperforms polished ads — can be systematized with AI. That's a contrarian position in a market where most AI content tools are still selling to bloggers who want to write faster. Yorby is aimed at the people who need content that converts: brands, agencies, and marketers who live and die by creative output.

Why the backgrounds matter

The founding team's backgrounds shape everything about how Yorby operates. Thomas Kim's time at Google gave the company deep technical credibility — when your co-founder built systems at one of the world's best engineering organizations, customers assume the infrastructure is solid. Meng's banking background, meanwhile, gives Yorby a fluency with business buyers that purely technical teams often lack.

That combination matters in the AI content market, where trust is the scarcest commodity. Every week brings a new AI writing tool, and buyers have learned to be skeptical. A team that can speak both the language of machine learning and the language of P&L statements has an edge in rooms where purchasing decisions get made. The $9.4k MRR figure suggests early customers are buying that story.

It's also worth noting the timing. The founders started Yorby as AI-generated content was crossing from novelty to infrastructure — the moment when businesses stopped asking 'is AI content any good?' and started asking 'how do we produce enough of it?' Building at that inflection point, with a team credible on both sides of the table, is the kind of positioning that's hard to replicate.

The AI UGC thesis

UGC — user-generated content — has become the dominant format in digital advertising. Consumers trust a shaky phone video from a 'real person' more than a polished brand spot, and performance marketers have known this for years. The problem has always been scale: authentic-feeling content is expensive and slow to produce.

That's the gap Yorby is attacking. If AI can generate content that carries the markers of authenticity — the casual framing, the conversational tone, the unpolished feel — then the economics of content marketing change completely. Meng's podcast appearance framed this as a structural shift rather than a feature: the future of content isn't brands making ads, it's systems producing an endless stream of creator-style assets.

The risk, of course, is that platforms and audiences learn to detect and discount AI-generated UGC, the same way they learned to ignore banner ads. Yorby's answer — implied by its positioning — is that quality and relevance beat detection. If the content works, the provenance matters less than the performance. It's a bet on pragmatism over purism, and the early revenue suggests at least some marketers agree.

There's also a second-order effect worth watching. As AI-generated content floods every channel, the premium on content that actually performs — measured in conversions, not impressions — goes up. Tools that optimize for output quality rather than output volume will separate from the pack. Yorby's positioning around UGC, the format with the highest trust premium in advertising, suggests the founders understand which game they're really playing.

What's next

Yorby is still early — $9.4k MRR is a foothold, not a victory lap. But the pieces are in place for something bigger: a credible founding team with complementary strengths, a clear thesis about where content is going, and early paying customers validating the direction.

The company's trajectory will likely depend on two things. First, whether AI-generated UGC maintains its performance edge as the format matures and audiences get savvier. Second, whether Yorby can move upmarket from early adopters to the brands and agencies that spend serious money on content. The founders' backgrounds suggest they understand both challenges.

For now, Yorby is a case study in how founding-team composition can be a go-to-market strategy in itself. In a market drowning in AI tools, being the team that buyers trust — because of where you've worked and what you understand about their business — might matter more than having the best model.

Whether Yorby becomes the default AI content layer for performance marketers or remains a promising early-stage tool depends on execution from here — but the founding thesis, the team, and the first $9.4k of proof are all real.

The playbook

  1. Use your pedigree as a wedge — Banking and Google alumni are a credibility shortcut with enterprise buyers and partners. Yorby's founders lean on it.
  2. Teach the market, don't just sell to it — The CEO went on the Frankly Speaking podcast early to explain the thesis publicly — content marketing that doubles as market validation.
  3. Ride a specific wave, not 'AI' in general — Yorby pairs AI generation with human-relevant output, riding the UGC wave rather than generic 'AI writing' claims.
  4. Pair a domain outsider with a technical insider — Two founders with complementary backgrounds (finance/ops and engineering) cover both the business and the build.
