What PeptIQ is about

What makes it different

PeptIQ is a peptide dose calculator and tracker for iPhone, Android and the web: work out your draw volume, track your vials, log every dose and see your upcoming schedule in one place. Blood-work uploads, journaling, a drug-interaction checker, educational guides and an AI assistant sit alongside the tracker, and the app charges a hard paywall of $30 a month or $130 a year.

It is the work of Kris Chase, a software engineer with 25 years of experience who works as a fractional CTO. PeptIQ was his first app built for himself rather than for clients: he spotted the gap while researching peptides, saw a hockey-stick curve on Google Search Trends, and built the first version in about a week in January 2026 using an AI-assisted coding tool. He had been programming since the MySpace era — one high-school project there made around $40,000 a month — but this was the first product that was his own.

The growth came from search, not ads. Chase wrote educational articles with useful tools embedded in them — a reconstitution calculator inside the guides, for example — and waited six to seven months before rankings materialised. Then they arrived: 590,000 clicks and 13.2 million impressions in twelve months, with Google driving about 70% of clicks and revenue. By November 2026 the app had crossed $115,000 in revenue for the year, with roughly 1,400 active subscriptions and about $14,500 in monthly recurring revenue — all without a single paid ad or influencer deal.
