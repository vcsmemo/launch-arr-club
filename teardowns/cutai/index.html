How CutAI Makes Money

CutAI sells time back to creators who film ten videos a day — an AI editor that turns a messy take into a postable cut in minutes — and it found its buyers in the App Store, the creator economy, and the founder's own feed.

1. What problem does it solve?

Filming is fast. Editing is the bottleneck. A UGC creator or TikTok Shop affiliate films ten or twenty short videos a day, and every one of them comes out messy — false starts, retakes, long pauses, filler words. Cutting that by hand takes ten to fifteen minutes per video, which means the editing queue, not the camera, decides how much a creator can post.

CutAI does the first cut for you. Upload a raw take from your camera roll and it removes the dead space, the retakes and the filler, picks the best take when you filmed several, writes the captions, and hands back a clean video — the site's own before-and-after shows a 1:10 raw take coming back as a 0:17 cut. You can queue up to ten videos at once, get a notification when they are ready, and export in up to 4K with no watermark. A real timeline editor sits underneath for the final touches.

The problem is not new — every creator has lived it — but the timing is. Short video is now the default format for selling things online, and the people who post the most videos win the most distribution. A tool that turns an hour of editing into a few minutes of review is not a nice-to-have for this buyer; it is the difference between three videos a day and ten.

2. Who pays, how much, and in what scenario?

The buyer: a creator who posts every day for a living — UGC creators making ads for brands, TikTok Shop affiliates filming product demos, and small content teams. The App Store reviews tell the story in their own words: "I make 10–20 videos a day. It used to take me hours. Now I am done in like 30 mins." "Fired my editor." "I was able to post 10 videos a day instead of 3."

Every plan starts with a 3-day free trial, and the same three prices apply as in-app purchases inside the iPhone app. The founder says the $49.99 Pro plan is the best seller — and the math backs that up.

The scenario that makes someone pay: the first week the queue wins. A creator who films ten videos a day burns through the Starter plan's 100 edits in ten days; Pro's 300 edits is the plan that actually covers a daily posting habit. The purchase decision is not "is this editor good" but "is $50 a month cheaper than the hours I lose editing" — and against a human editor, or against the revenue from seven extra videos a day, it is not a close call.

Sanity check (our estimate, not a reported figure): 10,000–12,000 paying subscribers at $556,462 MRR implies an average of roughly $46–$56 per subscriber per month — squarely in Pro-plan territory, exactly where the founder says the mix sits. The numbers are internally consistent, which is a good sign for a founder-reported figure that is only three months old.

3. How did it find these people?

Almost entirely through the store and the creators themselves. There is no sign of paid acquisition in the public record — no ad library footprint, no affiliate program, no launch campaign. What the record does show is three organic channels working together.

The App Store is the storefront. CutAI launched on the App Store in August 2026 and carries a 4.9-star rating. For an iPhone-only app sold to people who film on their phones, the store is both the discovery channel and the checkout: the buyer searches for an auto-editing app, reads reviews from creators like them, and subscribes without ever touching a website.

Creators sell it to creators. The site leads with creators who have 100,000+ followers, and the review base reads like a referral engine — TikTok Shop affiliates and UGC creators describing, in specific numbers, how many more videos they post now. When your product's output is content, every customer is a demo: a creator's followers see tighter videos, ask what changed, and arrive already convinced.

The founder builds in public. Keoni Thornhill announced the $556,462 MRR figure on X on November 25, 2026, in the same breath as the first-two-months revenue and the subscriber count. A milestone post is distribution in itself — it reaches other founders, other creators, and the press that covers creator-economy numbers — and it costs nothing but the willingness to publish the number.

4. Why does it work?

CutAI's wedge is not the AI — silence detection and auto-captioning are commodity features now. The wedge is the specific job, on the device where the job happens: a talking-head product video, filmed on an iPhone, that needs to be postable in minutes, not hours. Desktop editors are more powerful and irrelevant — the buyer never opens a laptop. Generic AI editors try to serve everyone; CutAI serves the person filming their tenth take of a lipstick demo in their kitchen.

Three details make the wedge hold. First, batching: queue ten videos, walk away, get notified — the product respects that its buyer films in bursts. Second, the trial: three days free on every plan means the buyer tests it on real videos before paying, and the value is visible in the first cut. Third, the price ladder matches the habit: 100 edits for the dabbler, 300 for the daily poster, unlimited for the shop running a team — each step up costs less than the revenue from the extra videos it unlocks.

It is also, unusually, a business with no visible funding and no visible team beyond the founder. That is not a weakness at this stage: an iPhone app with App Store distribution and a founder who posts the numbers has almost no fixed costs to cover before the revenue line turns into profit.

5. Could you build it?

The product, yes. The business, not by copying the product.

Replicable: the shape — transcribe the audio, cut the silence and the weak takes, add captions, export — can be assembled from existing speech and language APIs by a small team. The genuinely hard product work is the judgment: which take is the best one, and what counts as a filler worth removing. That is a taste problem as much as a technical one, and it is where the 4.9-star rating is earned or lost.

Not replicable by cloning: the distribution. A 4.9-star App Store rating built in three months, a review base of creators quoting their own output numbers, and a founder willing to publish revenue in public. A new entrant would need its own trust channel — a different creator niche, a different platform, a different community — not a better silence detector.

The opening it leaves: Android. The site answers "Is CutAI on Android?" with a flat "Not yet." The same job exists for every Android creator, and whoever serves them first gets the same App Store-style compounding on Google Play. Teams and agencies are the second opening: the pricing is built for one creator, not for a shop running five accounts — and the Ultimate plan's "unlimited" hints the founder already sees that demand coming.

What we don't know

  • Churn and refund rates — the two numbers that decide whether $556K MRR is a plateau or a peak. A 3-day trial on every plan makes trial-to-paid conversion the number to watch, and it is unpublished.
  • Whether the reported MRR is gross or net of Apple's App Store commission, and how subscriptions split between in-app purchase and the website's own checkout.
  • Gross margin after Apple and AI inference costs — batch video processing is not free, and the unlimited plan is the one most exposed to heavy users.
  • Team size, burn, and whether any of the first-two-months $1M+ was one-off (annual prepayments, launch pricing) rather than recurring.
  • How much of the growth is App Store search vs. creator word of mouth vs. the founder's own posts — the mix decides how defensible the growth is.
