How traxy.ai Makes Money

traxy.ai sells timing — the minutes after someone engages on LinkedIn — and it found its buyers inside the founder's own agency, a business twice its size. $54.6K MRR, 240 subscriptions, founded January 2026.

1. What problem does it solve?

LinkedIn is where B2B deals start, but the signal is perishable. Someone likes your post, comments on a competitor's launch, or follows a company in your market — and that moment of intent is gone within hours if nobody moves on it. Sales teams know this. They still lose the lead, because nobody sits watching likes and comments all day.

traxy.ai watches instead. Every hour it scans likes, comments, and posts across a target market, scores each person against the customer's ideal customer profile, and hands sales teams a lead list with the engagement behind every name — the like, the comment, the post, the profile view. The list routes to a CRM, an outreach tool, or Slack, and traxy runs LinkedIn and email sequences on autopilot from the customer's own connected accounts. It can also be called from AI assistants like Claude and ChatGPT through MCP.

The problem is old (speed-to-lead: the first seller to a warm prospect usually wins). The new part is that the listening, the scoring, and the first outreach can now run without a human in the loop.

2. Who pays, how much, and in what scenario?

The buyer: a B2B founder or sales team already doing LinkedIn outbound by hand — and losing warm leads to slow follow-up. Plus agencies that run traxy for their clients from one account.

The mix must include meaningful Advanced, Scale, and agency spend, or add-ons. MRR annualizes to roughly $655,000 a year. One wrinkle worth watching: last-30-days cash was $48,900, below the $54,600 MRR — cash collected trails the run rate, which is normal timing, but it is the number to check next month.

3. How did it find these people?

Almost entirely through the founder's own agency. Ben Buaron, 27, runs Windmill Growth, a done-for-you LinkedIn ghostwriting agency for B2B founders — more than 185 clients served, about 30 on the books at a time, doing about $120,000 a month of its own recurring revenue. The agency is roughly twice the size of the product it spawned.

The product was born from a churn investigation, and Buaron wrote it up in public. In April 2026 he published a post titled "Three months ago — I almost shut down Windmill": good clients were leaving, and he could not figure out why. Days inside CRM notes, call recordings, and engagement logs gave him the answer. Some clients closed deals consistently from LinkedIn; others posted great content every week and got nothing back. Same effort, different results. It was not a content problem. It was a timing problem: the clients winning were the ones who moved the moment someone liked or commented — DM, follow-up, meeting, deal. Fast, clean, every time. The clients losing waited for inbound that never came.

So he built a system internally — the moment someone engages, identify whether they match the ideal customer profile, instantly — and it worked for Windmill first, then for every client they gave it to. "A few months later, we turned that system into a product. Because this wasn't a Windmill problem." The audience was already there: agency clients who pay for LinkedIn growth are exactly the people who would pay for the tool that does the follow-up. Early access opened to non-clients with a 50%-off code for the first 10.

He also built in public. On April 15, 2026, day 32 of his build-in-public series, he reported 100 active users and 13 paying users — about three new users a day. There is no paid acquisition anywhere in the record. The marketing budget was the agency's client list and the founder's own LinkedIn presence.

4. Why does it work?

traxy.ai's wedge is not the AI — social listening is a crowded market, and the pieces (engagement tracking, enrichment, sequencing) are all buyable. The wedge is the insight plus the channel: a timing rule learned from real churn data inside a working agency, sold to the exact buyers that agency already serves. The product is the agency's own workflow, productized — the classic agency-to-tool path, and the reason it reached $54,600 MRR in about ten months with a team of two to five and no funding. The lead list itself helps too: every name arrives with the engagement behind it, so it reads as researched rather than scraped, which is the difference between a list a rep trusts and one they ignore.

5. Could you build it?

The product, yes. The business, not by copying the product.

Replicable: the shape — watch engagement across a market, score against an ideal profile, enrich, sequence — can be assembled from existing APIs and enrichment providers by a small team. The pricing shape is copyable too: leads as the metered unit, senders as the add-on.

Not replicable by cloning: the distribution. A clone launched now enters a crowded market without an agency full of paying LinkedIn customers to sell to first, and without the churn analysis that produced the timing insight. You would need your own trust channel — a different audience that already pays you for the outcome — not a better listening agent.

The opening it leaves: the last month. MRR has moved sideways — $54,553 on October 19, a $56,000 peak on November 18, $54,600 on November 21. The agency channel got it to $55K; the question is what gets it past $55K. Whoever answers that — a second channel, a second product surface, or a bigger agency motion — owns the next leg.

What we don't know

  • Retention and churn — the numbers that decide whether a flat month is a pause or a leak. MRR has barely moved in a month ($54,553 → $54,600) while subscriptions held at 240; whether new sales are offsetting churn is undisclosed.
  • The plan mix — the ~$228 average implies real Advanced/Scale/agency revenue, but how much of the $54,600 is the $149 tier vs. the higher tiers is not disclosed.
  • Whether last-30-days cash ($48,900) trailing MRR is timing, failed payments, or annual-plan effects — the number to watch next month.
  • Platform risk — LinkedIn restricts automation and scraping, and traxy tracks engagement and sends from connected accounts. How the product stays inside LinkedIn's rules, and what happens if enforcement tightens, is not addressed in any public source we found.
  • Costs and margins — no founder-stated costs for traxy.ai; MRR is not profit.
  • Whether the $56,000 peak on November 18 or the $54,600 level holds — the figures move daily, so re-check before quoting.
